# Design

## The loop

Passengers are the resource. Each owned station generates trips to every station a train takes them to, by how long the journey takes door to door (Journeys, below) and the demand curve (`demandCurve` in `src/sim/sim.ts`: morning peak, evening peak, nothing at night). They join a queue on the platform for their direction, a group at a time, each with its destination and the time it gives up. They board the train for their direction while it stands (changing at Stockport if their journey runs between the main line and the Hazel Grove branch), through its doors at a set rate, and pay a distance-based fare as they get on; whoever the doors can't take in the stop waits for the next, and are counted off at their destination (`alighted`, shown as Arrived). A full platform turns new arrivals away; waiting past their patience sends them home. Both count as lost, unless a taxi rank takes them (Taxi ranks, below).

## Places

Everything the player can buy or grow is a **place** (`src/sim/places.ts`; the design in `docs/design/buying.md`), by id `kind:name`: a station (`station:stockport`), a section of line between neighbouring stations or junctions (`section:main:heaton-chapel:levenshulme`), a junction (`junction:edgeley`), a route, a train service (`route:stopper`), a stop, a bus stop or bus station by a station (`stop:levenshulme-station-view`, `stops` in the network data), and a depot (`depot:stockport-sidings`, `depots`). Stations and stops are bought; the rest come with what's owned: the stopper from the start, a branch's service once a station beyond its home is (as it runs), the sidings with Stockport, a section once both its ends are (a junction end, a station beyond it), a junction with any section through it.

- **Touching** works from the network: along each corridor its stations and junctions in chainage order, a corridor joining at its end, so the next station each way through junctions (`near`). A station is offered when a neighbouring station is owned (the services running past don't count) and its `needs` are built; a stop when its station is owned (`offered`).
- **Stages** are on the places they belong to (`stagesFor`): a station's from `UPGRADES` (the ones its `grow` lists, and the everyday ones); the stopper's units and longer trains, and the sidings' spare unit, from `LINE_UPGRADES`; a section's line speed (The line between stations, below). Junctions and stops have none yet (the line between stations, and buses as a network, add them). Prices, prerequisites and effects are as they were; `Sim.buy(place, stage?)` pays and builds, and `buyStation`, `buyUpgrade` and `buyLine` are the same buys by station index or line upgrade, as the view asks for them. `bus` in `UPGRADES` is the stop by the station (`stop: true`), kept so the sim and the bot read it as a stage; the view leaves it off the station's card and shows the stop as its own place.
- **The saved game** holds `places`: each owned or grown place with its stages built (a count). The sim works out `owned`, `ups` (with `bus` once the stop by the station is owned) and `line` from it (`derive`) as fixed values that aren't saved, so the view reads them as before; to change what's owned a test or check uses `Sim.grant`.

## Journeys

People travel when the whole journey fits their time budget: about an hour a day, half an hour each way (Marchetti's constant), and further when transport gets faster. `src/sim/journeys.ts` works out every station to every other, door to door, whenever something it depends on changes (a purchase, units in service), in sim seconds, which are real ones:

- **Walk** at each end: 8 minutes for the near side; the far side (half the catchment) walks 8 more round to a crossing until the station has a way across, then 3 more over the stairs, and none with lifts, which also take a minute off everyone's.
- **Wait**: half the gap between trains that way (the service's round trip over its units).
- **Ride**: each stretch between calls from a stand to a stand at line speed, plus a dwell at each stop between.
- **Change** wherever the quickest way needs one (Stockport, between the branch and the main line, today): the next wait, and five felt minutes more. A trip is a path through the graph of rides on services and changes between them, found by Dijkstra on felt time (`journeys`, its `legs` and the stations it changes `at`), as many changes as that takes; a change is always onto another service, so nobody rides past and comes back on the same line. Someone setting out waits on the platform for the way their trip's first ride goes (`dirIdx`).

People feel walking and waiting at twice riding (UK appraisal practice), waiting at 1.5 under a canopy, and a crowded train's ride up to 1.6 times as long (rising from 60% full). Trips from station i to station j an hour (`tripRates`) are `demand × curve(hour) × reach × (out × catchment_i × draw_j + (1 − out) × draw_i × catchment_j) / draw of Piccadilly`: homes at one end and what draws people at the other (`catchment` and `draw` in the network data, from Census 2021: Homes and draw, below), homes being each station's homes now (Homes that grow, below), with `out` the share leaving home (0.8 before noon, 0.35 after). Reach falls smoothly around the budget, `1 / (1 + e^((felt − 60 min) / 12 min))`: half of people's budgets are over an hour felt (about half an hour actual), fewer travel further, and nobody is cut off.

### Homes and draw

Each station's `catchment` (homes) and `draw` (work) come from Census 2021, by `tools/census-to-network.mjs` (12 October 2026), which writes them and a note into `src/data/network.json`. It reads usual residents (TS001) and the workplace population (WP001) by Output Area, placed at each area's population-weighted centroid (the ONS Open Geography Portal), and every National Rail station's position (NaPTAN). `--fetch` downloads them into the git-ignored `data/raw/` and writes `data/census/extract.json` (the areas near the line and the stations round them), which the tool then runs on offline.

- **Homes:** each area's residents go to the station whose trip into Manchester is best door to door, walking there (at most 1.5 km, felt at twice riding) and riding on, counting every real station, so Reddish North, Gorton or Mauldeth Road take their own. Beyond a walk of every station, areas within 5 km of Stockport take the bus to it: its bus station stands beside it, which is most of why it's far busier than the stations up the line.
- **Draw:** each area's workers go to the station nearest on foot, within 1 km. Until phase 6 brings Oxford Road, Deansgate, Victoria and Salford Central, Piccadilly stands for the whole city centre and takes their areas, homes and work.
- **Scale:** one factor for homes (0.0115) keeps Stockport's morning trips to Piccadilly as the old guesses gave them, so the opening and its first peak hold; draw is scaled so Piccadilly's stays 6,000 (the sim only uses draw over Piccadilly's). Rounded to tens.
- **What moved:** Stockport's draw is half the old guess (730 against 1,400) and Piccadilly's homes well under it (830 against 2,200), so fewer travel out of Stockport in the evening; `fareMult` went from 12 to 13.5 to keep Levenshulme in reach before the first night. Ardwick has real homes now (160 against 12), the stations up the line draw more (Heaton Chapel 520 against 120, Woodsmoor 750 with Stepping Hill Hospital), and Hazel Grove has fewer homes (170 against 300).
- **Ceiling:** how many homes each station's land could hold (`ceiling`): its homes, and half as many again as live within a walk of it (1.5 km, every area whichever station it takes now: infill, flats on old yards, new estates), on the same scale. Homes that grow, below, grow towards it.
- **Pools:** the rest of the areas beyond a walk of every station come once a car park or bus link is built (Car parks and bus links, above). Half of an area's residents drive (a game choice), to the nearest of the line's stations within 8 km that's nearer Manchester than they are and at least 4 km from Piccadilly (Piccadilly's car park takes the city's own drivers within 3 km); the other half take the bus, to the nearest station if it's the line's and within 3 km, or else to Stockport's Interchange within 7 km. Each resident counted once, at one station; times are the straight line times 1.3 at 8 m/s driving and 5 m/s by bus. The extract now holds the areas within 8 km of the line's stations (7 km of Stockport); homes, draw and the ceiling still come only from those within 2.5 km (5 km of Stockport), as before.
- **Caveats:** the census was taken in March 2021, in lockdown, when people working from home were counted at home, so the city centre's jobs are understated against a normal year; buses and trams are not counted as competition except for Stockport's own buses.

### Homes that grow

Where the line brings places within reach, people move there (Marchetti's constant). Each station's homes (`homes` in the saved game) start at its census `catchment` and, while it's owned, grow towards its `ceiling` (`grow` in `src/sim/sim.ts`): each six hours by the clock (a sim hour at the peaks' rate) they close `TUNING.grow` (6%) of the gap, times the reach of the trip to where its people mostly go (`Sim.destination`, as the glow on the map uses: Piccadilly, or from Piccadilly the station a train reaches with the biggest draw), `1 / (1 + e^((felt − 60 min) / 12 min))` before crowding. A halt whose trip feels about the hour grows at half the rate; Stockport and the stations up the line, once they have their way across and a second unit, at about three quarters; the Hazel Grove branch, with its change and its one unit, at about a third; whatever saves minutes (a way across, more units, the faster timetable) makes it quicker. So it's slow: about a sixth of the gap in a game day at a good station, more than half in five; on the bot's line about 130 homes in two game days, 260 in three and a half (8 sim hours). Nothing random: the same purchases give the same homes. It's stepped with the sim, so a session away is caught up with the rest on load. Demand reads the homes (`tripRates`: homes at the home end, morning and evening), so growth pays the player back in fares.

On the map (`src/render/homes.ts`, view only) new terraces and small blocks of flats stand round each station, one building for every 3 homes over its catchment, nearest the station first, so they fill the reach's glow: flats mostly within about 300 m (and at Ardwick and Piccadilly), terraces and pairs further out, in brick, buff and pastel render (the `--map-home-*` tokens) under slate or tile roofs. Where they can go is worked out at start from a seed of the station's own: 150 to 780 m from it, clear of its kit (210 m), the tracks, the railway's land (every station's pad and the tops of its walls, with 10 m to spare), the roads, the river, the M60, the viaduct's valley and the houses already drawn (`World.buildable`), and again once the real buildings are in (The surroundings): then they take the real gaps first, nearest the station: OSM's brownfield, building land and greenfield, and open ground with no building or tree (not a park, pitch, wood, water, yard, car park or cemetery); where the ring has no more room, further out to 1.2 km, on real gaps still, then any clear ground there. With today's line in, the share of each station's plots on a real gap within its ring: Heaton Chapel, Ardwick and Piccadilly all, Davenport 93%, Levenshulme 92%, Woodsmoor 82%, Hazel Grove 70%, Stockport 60%; the rest stand further out (`HomesLayer.gaps`, on `window.__view.homes`). The Buxton line's stations have no tiles yet, so all their ground counts as open. A new one rises into place as a bought part does (at once with reduced motion); a loaded save shows its homes at once. Instanced: walls, pitched roofs, flat roofs and window bands, matrices written only when one appears or while it rises.

Patience is the budget left. Each group draws its own budget from the people who'd make that journey at all (the reach curve is how budgets spread), and gives up once its wait has used what's left after the walks, the ride and any change, never sooner than three minutes. Waiting up to a whole timetabled gap between trains is felt as waiting is (twice riding, 1.5 under a canopy); past that, a late train or one that went by full, it's felt at three times riding (`delayWeight`, as UK appraisal weighs lateness; a canopy takes the same quarter off), so delays use up budgets fast. So people whose trip only just fits give up first, and long waits bite at halts with long walks and one train each way. Someone changing trains doesn't turn back.

**The reach on the map** (`src/render/reach.ts`, view only). A warm glow on the ground round each owned station over the homes from which the trip to where its people mostly go (the station a train reaches with the biggest `draw`: Piccadilly, or from Piccadilly, Stockport) fits the budget: its radius is how far a walk from home can start, at 70 m a minute as the crow flies, while the journey (`Sim.journey`, before crowding) still feels no longer than an hour. The sim's walk is the average one, 8 minutes, so the reach is `70 m × ((60 min − felt) / 2 + 8 min)`: a long wait or a slow ride shrinks it, and a station whose trip feels well over the hour (the Hazel Grove branch with its change and its one unit) has none. It's full well inside and fades over its last third, the edge a touch brighter, and a little light of its own so it shows at dusk; a locked station has none. One shader patch on the terrain draws it, its targets worked out again only when the journeys change (`journeyKey`), easing out to them over about a second (at once with reduced motion). A card's preview draws a cyan ghost ring where each station's reach would be once it's bought (only where it moves), and the card says what it saves where it saves minutes on those trips ("3 min less walk for about 1,000 homes"; waits that only feel shorter under a canopy say so): from a copy of the sim with it bought, never stepped, homes being the homes now (the census homes, grown) of the stations that save, each saving weighted by them. The Minutes control beside Whole line shades the ground by real minutes to Manchester instead (the station's own part of the walk, the wait, the ride and the far walk, plus the walk from home to the nearest station that does best; Piccadilly's surroundings walk in), in four bands ending at 20, 30, 40 and 50 minutes, with a key; it's off by default.

## Timetable

Trains run to a timetable (`depart`, `timetable` and `busy` in `src/sim/sim.ts`). Each unit carries `due`, when it's timetabled to leave the stop it's at or running to, and `late`, how far past that it is (live while it stands, and as it left its last stop while it runs), so the departure boards of phase 4 can read it straight off the train. Leaving a stop, a train plans its next departure from when it was due away, not when it left: the run there at line speed (`runTime`, from a stand to a stand) and the planned stop there, 45 s (`dwell`), or at the end of its run the turnaround, 120 s (`turnaround`, plus 30 s with the faster timetable, `recoveryFast`). So lateness carries on down the line. At the end of its run a unit also keeps the headway behind the last unit to turn there (the service's planned round trip over its units, `headway`, from the same running times the journeys use), so units added mid-line spread out to an even gap within a round trip.

A train never leaves before it's due. It leaves once it has stood its least (30 s at a stop, `dwellMin`; 60 s to turn round, `turnMin`), it's due, and nobody is still getting on or off: everyone off first (each takes half a boarder's door time, `alight`), then the queue its way boards through the doors, slower as the train fills (to 30% of the door rate when full, `doorsCrowd`), and the doors close 15 s (`dispatch`) after the platform first clears; anyone who arrives meanwhile still gets on. A full train with a crowd still on the platform is held a second for each person left trying the doors (`squeeze`). Nothing holds a train past its least plus two minutes (`overrun`). The difference between the planned and the least stand is recovery: a late train makes up 15 s at each quiet stop and a minute (90 s with the faster timetable) at each end. A train never closes within 400 m (`separation`) of the one ahead of it on its line; it brakes to stand behind, and it waits at a signal for a platform (Platforms, below).

**Last and first trains.** The stoppers run from the first train, 05:30, to the last, 23:30, from either end (`hours` on the services in the network data; `dawn`, `leaves` and `stabled` in `src/sim/sim.ts`). A unit due away from the end of its run after the last train stands there overnight instead, and the timetable's ends start fresh each morning: the first unit to stand at an end leaves with the first train and each after it the headway behind, so nothing bunches at dawn. A service with a home (the Hazel Grove line) runs out from Stockport to stand at its far end, as the real units stable at Hazel Grove; the main line's stand in Piccadilly's bays and at Stockport, drawn overnight in Stockport's carriage sidings (the roads beyond the spare's and the perk's unit's, `src/render/trains.ts`; the sim keeps them at their platform, which nothing else needs then). Nobody waits through the night: demand is nil from 00:00 to 04:00, and anyone who comes after the main line's last train gives up as they would at a full platform, counted as lost (about ten a run on the bot's line); the boards show the first train's time overnight. The quick night (23:00 to 05:00) passes in fewer sim seconds than a run along the line, so the last trains are still finishing their runs through most of it and stand only briefly before the first; that's the clock, not the timetable (a game choice to keep the real times).

Lateness spreads by itself: a late train finds more people waiting at its next stops, so it stands longer and gets later, while the one behind finds fewer; and every minute late is a minute of waiting that uses up people's budgets (Journeys), so a late train loses people at every stop. Longer trains ease it most (more doors, and the train is less full so they board quicker); extra units shorten the gaps, so smaller crowds. On the bot's line it shows at the second morning's peak before the longer trains (a stressed line runs up to three minutes late), and hardly at all after.

**Platforms.** Every platform the player builds carries trains (`platforms` on the stations and the services in `src/data/network.json`, and `platsFor`, `claim`, `holds` and `stopAt` in `src/sim/sim.ts`). A station's platforms are its data's (Stockport's 0 to 4, Piccadilly's 1 to 12), each with the number the boards show, the stage it comes with, and where its track runs (a network track along it, or the rails drawn into a bay); a through station without a list has the halt's platform (1) and the second platform (2), one each way. A service lists the platforms it uses at a station, calling north, calling south or turning round, preferred first: the stopper turns at Stockport in 4, 3 (the second-platform stage) and the bay 0 (with platforms 0, 1 and 2), and at Piccadilly in 5 and, from the start since the railway's station is fully built, the bays 11 and 12; the Hazel Grove line in Stockport's 1; the expresses use Stockport's 3 north and 2 south, and Piccadilly's 6 and 7 (5 while the player owns it without the shed). A turning list is shared out by unit, so the first unit tries the first platform and the second the next: with two units the stoppers turn in 4 and 3 by turns, which is what the owner described. A train claims a platform 700 m out (`claim`): its first choice that nobody holds, then the next. One train at a platform at a time: a platform is held by a train standing there, one running in that has claimed it, and one leaving until it has run 150 m on (`clear`, its tail off the platform). A train with no platform free stands at its signal, 450 m short of the stop (`approach`), until one is; at a station the player doesn't own (Piccadilly), the railway's, it takes its first choice regardless, so it never holds a train. A train stands centred on the station's stop, or the platform's own (`stop`: Stockport's bay 0 is short, so a unit stands at its north end). The platform each train holds is saved with it (`Train.pf`, `pfAt` and `bound`). The platform stages relieve pressure you can see: platform 3 lets the expresses call, and they share it with the stoppers' second unit, so a stopper can wait at the signal behind one; the bay 0 gives a third unit a platform of its own; before the shed at an owned Piccadilly, the expresses take the stopper's 5. Which platform each service uses, and where it's a game choice, is in the network data's `platformNote`.

Upgrades save minutes where that's what they do: the way across and lifts cut the walk, a canopy makes the wait feel shorter, more units cut the wait, the faster timetable cuts the ride and the round trip. The rest of Stockport and of Piccadilly still adds 20% to the catchment.

Money buys places and their stages (Places, below): the next stations, their stages, the stopper's units, longer trains and faster timetable, and the bus stops and bus station by the stations. A station is offered once a station next to it along the lines is owned (`offerable` in `src/sim/sim.ts`, `touches` in `src/sim/places.ts`), so not strictly in order: owning Stockport offers both Heaton Chapel and, across Edgeley Junction, Davenport; Ardwick offers Ashburys across Ardwick Junction; Hazel Grove offers Middlewood. A station can also wait on something elsewhere (`needs` in the network data: Davenport waits on Stockport's platforms 0, 1 and 2, where the Hazel Grove line turns round), standing fogged with its price until then. The bot saves for the offered station that brings most homes and draw per pound, counting what it waits on, which on today's network buys Heaton Chapel, Levenshulme, Ardwick, Piccadilly, Davenport, Woodsmoor and Hazel Grove in that order, then the Marple line ahead of Middlewood.

Every station starts as a halt, Stockport included: one platform on one line, so only trains one way call (towards Manchester, or at Piccadilly the trains leaving) and only passengers going that way come; nobody can travel to a halt the other way, unless a train turns round there. It grows through stages, each an upgrade with prerequisites (`requires` in `UPGRADES`), named per station in the network data's `grow`, and offered only where `grow` lists it: a way across the line (+15% catchment; Woodsmoor's is steps to the road), the second platform (trains both ways, and longer platforms go after it; at Stockport it's platform 3, and the line's extra units wait for it, since a second train needs room to turn), the booking office (90% of fares instead of 82%, and its kiosk's £0.35 a boarding passenger: the kiosk folded into it at the price of both, £4,500, part 13d; barriers, shops and the car park go after it, and at Piccadilly it's the concourse), a taxi rank where the real station has a rank or a forecourt (Taxi ranks, below), and at Stockport and Piccadilly the rest of the station (+20% catchment). A need a station doesn't offer counts as met. The station's card's figures are its Homes (the homes now, Homes that grow), Waiting, Boarded, Arrived, Revenue, Gave up, Changed, and By cab where it has a rank; how many went by replacement bus (`trouble.bused`, line-wide) is on the stopper's card and the Network card. The card shows what's ready to build as a card each (its price, what it adds, and what it opens next; Buy once the money is there, or a meter and how much to go), and what's built on one line; the rest stays hidden until it's reachable. Tapping a card previews it on the map: the parts it adds drawn as a ghost in blueprint cyan (`--map-ghost`, apart from the passengers' amber; the chosen card's edge matches) and the camera on them (`previewUpgrade` and `partsBox` in `src/render/stations.ts`, `frame` in `src/render/camera.ts`). Buying it makes them rise into place in about a second (at once with reduced motion), and a bought station's halt rises out of the fog the same way. Tapping a chip on the Built line puts the camera on what that step built, with no ghost. A card's `tags` row takes badges (a stage now). The stopper's card shows its stages the same way (the sidings' card the spare unit): the next step of each as a card ("Off-peak timetable", the step after it under Then), what's bought on a Built line, extra units hidden until Stockport's platform 3. Choosing one of those cards, or its Built chip, frames a main-line unit (the newest, once one's just been bought; otherwise the one nearest Stockport) for units and cars; choosing a station's card instead clears it. The faster timetable (every section the stopper runs at its raised line speed, The line between stations, below) has its own work on the map (`src/render/lineside.ts`), showing with the last of those sections' steps: line-speed boards reading 90 where boards reading 75 stood, at every other one of the network's signal sites on the main line (a site every 440 m, halfway between two masts and clear of the stations). The colour-light signals come with each section's resignalling. 

## Taxi ranks

A stage offered where the real station has a rank or a forecourt cabs wait on (Stockport's on Station Road, Heaton Chapel, Levenshulme, Piccadilly and Hazel Grove; `grow` names it), £1,200. A rank has six cabs (`cabs`). When a party gives up at a station with a rank (turned away by a full platform, or out of patience), each of them takes a cab with a chance of 60% (`cabShare`) while there's one in: a cab takes up to four going the same way (`cabSeats`) and is away three minutes (`cabRank`) plus the ride there and back at two minutes a mile (`cabMile`, town traffic, in real seconds like everything that moves), so a busy peak empties the rank and the rest still give up. The player takes half the cab fare, which is about three times the train's, so 1.5 times the train fare a passenger (`cabCut`). They count as taken by cab (`StationState.cab`, `stats.cab`), not lost. `StationState.cabs` holds when each cab is back (free once that's passed; empty until a rank is built), and `Sim.cabsIn` counts those in. On the bot's line Stockport's rank, bought just after the second unit, earns about £1,500 over the rest of the 8 hours; Heaton Chapel's and Levenshulme's, bought in the second morning, a few hundred each; about 30 people go by cab, a fifth of those who give up, and lost falls from about 200 to 140.

On the map (`src/render/taxis.ts`, the kit part `taxi`): tarmac with a yellow-marked bay and a kerb, a shelter and a TAXI sign, and the cabs nose to tail with their For Hire lights lit. Read from the sim twice a second: when one fewer cab is in, someone walks from the station's way out (`door`) to the front cab and it pulls away, light off, and the rest move up; a cab back from a fare drives in at the back. All out, the rank stands empty. Cabs and people are instanced; at 10× or after a jump the rank goes straight to how many are in.

## Car parks and bus links

The ground round a station brings people from beyond a walk (`src/sim/access.ts`). Each station's `access` in the network data (from the census, Homes and draw, below) gives two pools of homes beyond a walk of every station: those who'd drive here and park (`drive`, their drive in seconds `driveTime`, and `driveR`, the distance nine in ten live within) and those who'd come by bus (`bus`, `busTime`, `busR`); Stockport's `busShare` is the share of its own homes that already come by bus (its 5 km bus pool, 78%). Nobody from a pool travels until the player builds the stage that brings them:

- **Car parks** (`carpark`, and a bigger `carpark2` where the real one is big enough to stage): Stockport's multi-storey (40 spaces, £18,000; an upper deck of 40 more, £20,000), Levenshulme's off Station View (25, £10,000), Piccadilly's multi-storey (80, £35,000; the city's own drivers going out) and Hazel Grove's (40, £16,000; an extension of 40, £18,000). Spaces and prices are in each station's `grow`. Drivers travel as everyone does (`tripRates`, homes at the home end), their journey felt with the drive (as riding) and a 3-minute walk from the car park (`parkWalk`, at twice riding) in place of the 8-minute walk from home. One to a car; each car takes a space until `parkStay` (9) clock hours later and pays `parkCharge` (£20). Once it's full the rest don't come, and aren't counted lost. `StationState.parked` holds the cars by the clock hour they leave (24; empty until a car park is built), `car` and `stats.car` who came by car.
- **Bus links** (`bus`, each a stop of its own in the network data's `stops`: Places, above): Stockport Interchange (£50,000), a stop on Station View at Levenshulme (£6,000), on the station approach at Hazel Grove (£2,000), and at Disley, New Mills Newtown, Romiley, Marple and Rose Hill (£3,000), where the census gives a pool. A bus rider's journey is felt with the bus ride (as riding), a 6-minute wait for it (`busWait`, felt as waiting), a 2-minute walk and a 5-minute change onto the train (`busChange`). At Stockport the Interchange makes the change 4 felt minutes quicker and covered (`busSave`) for its own riders and for the homes that already come by bus, whose extra trips (those that now fit) are counted as by bus too. `StationState.bus`, `stats.bus`.
- Their way home in the evening isn't counted yet, as with Piccadilly's homes standing for the city. Heaton Chapel's, Davenport's and Woodsmoor's surroundings beyond a walk are almost all within 5 km of Stockport, so the census already sends them by bus to Stockport, and they have no bus link; Heaton Chapel and Davenport have drive pools but no car park drawn yet.

On the bot's line a car park at a busy station pays for itself in about two to three game days and fills every morning (Levenshulme's by 07:30; Stockport's first 40 by midday); see the `balance` playbook's runs. A car arriving in the 8 sim hours is mostly the second morning; over 16 sim hours Stockport's car park brings about 300 drivers and Levenshulme's about 190, and Levenshulme's bus stop about 200.

On the map the `carpark` parts (already drawn as scenery) are gated on the stage and fill as the sim's car park does (`src/render/life.ts`: every drawn stage at the share of spaces taken). A `buslink` part (`src/render/links.ts`) is a row of bays with a shelter and flag each, and for Stockport Interchange its hall with Viaduct Park on the roof, placed at its real distance from the station; `LinkLayer` runs the town's buses in Greater Manchester yellow (`--map-link-bus`), into each bay every twelve clock minutes (five in the peaks), off the sim's clock, instanced. Parts away from the platforms carry `street: true`: they stand on the ground under them (`src/render/stations.ts`; a rank or a stop on the highest ground along its bay, so neither end sinks), seated again whenever a fine terrain tile comes in (`groundVersion`), and are kept off the station's pad (`partBoxes` in `src/render/terrain.ts`); `town()` keeps its filler blocks off a bus station's hall. The reach (`src/render/reach.ts`) draws a fainter outer ring, a pale wash with a thin edge, out to where a car park or bus link reaches (`accessReach`: its pool's `driveR` or `busR`, while one in ten of them still find the trip fits), and a card's preview draws its ghost. The station panel shows By car and By bus beside By cab.

## The car

The car is the rival the player can see (`docs/design/gridlock.md` §2). `src/sim/traffic.ts` works the roads out from what the sim already knows, and nothing in `Sim.step` reads it, so play is the same with or without it (the bot's `PLAY` is unchanged). Its data, `src/data/traffic.json`, is passed in rather than imported, so the page can load it after the first frame.

- **Who drives.** `wouldTravel(i, j)` is `tripRates` without the reach: everyone who'd make the trip at all, with each station's homes now. Whoever the network doesn't carry (one less the reach of their journey; nobody where either end is shut) drives, `carShare` (0.72, Stockport's share driving to work, Census 2021) of them, one to a car. A station's drive pool drives the whole way until its car park is built, then only to it. For a steady figure the journey is felt uncrowded and before the platforms' bonus.
- **Where.** Each stretch is a real DfT count point (2024 average daily flow) with the pairs of places whose drive uses it (`serves`: station ids or `corridor:<id>`, with a share where the drive fans out over other roads). Capacity is the peak hour (9% of the day) over 0.95. `carScale` (0.25) turns the game's people into real cars; the traffic the network never touches (round Manchester, deliveries) is set at load so each stretch stands at its `start` load at 08:00 with nothing running (1.25 on the A6 north of Stockport, 1.2 elsewhere), and follows a real count's shape through the day (`profile`, the A57 Hyde Road's).
- **What it gives.** `traffic(sim, TUNING, data, hour)`: each stretch's cars an hour, load, speed (`free / (1 + 0.15 × load⁴)`, a crawl in a queue), queue (grown by the excess over capacity from 05:00 in ten-minute steps, drained under it) and the drive along it now against when clear; the cars over them all, the same with nothing running, and the cars off the road at 08:00. `trafficKey` says when to work it out again.
- **Calibrated** so that at 08:00 today's opening has the A6 at 1.11 to 1.17, the main line to Piccadilly with everything bought brings it to 0.95, and the M60 stays at about 1.2 (most of its traffic goes round Manchester). The bot prints the cars at the first and last morning peaks, cars off % (of every car, through traffic and all: 2 to 3% at 8 sim hours, since the M60 is most of the cars), drives off % (of the drives the network could take: 25 to 28%), the A6 at Mersey Square's and the M60's loads at the last peak (0.98 to 1.01 and 1.19 at 8 sim hours), first fare s (220 today) and first buy, and a table of every stretch's load at each morning peak. Proposed ranges once the gridlock opening (part G2) lands: drives off 20 to 40% at 8 sim hours, the A6 0.9 to 1.05.

## Things going wrong

A few clear kinds of trouble, seeded like everything else (`src/sim/trouble.ts`, `GameState.trouble`). Nothing goes wrong in the first six hours by the clock (`troubleFrom`), then on average once every 15 clock hours (`troubleEvery`, 2.5 sim hours at the peaks' rate), never within four clock hours of the last one ending (`troubleGap`; these gaps go by the clock, `later` in `src/sim/day.ts`, so trouble comes as often in a game day however fast the clock runs), one at a time, and only between 06:00 and 21:00 by the game's clock, so a run of bad luck can't pile up and it happens where the player sees it. Nothing starts where it would still be going in a peak's busiest hour (`troubleCalm`: 06:30–08:30 and 16:00–18:00 by the clock, since a failure lasts about an hour by it), so bad luck is a hiccup, never a wrecked morning: on the bot's seeds 1–12 no run earns more than about a twelfth less than the same seed with no trouble. Half are failed trains, half signal failures (`failShare`).

- **A failed train.** A unit running between two stations, at least 300 m from either (`failMargin`), stops where it is with its hazard lights flashing and blocks its line one way until it's rescued, about 9 minutes (`rescue`, give or take a quarter; 4 with a spare unit, `rescueSpare`). Trains behind it close up to 400 m and wait, and the ones that are held run late afterwards and make it up as late trains do (Timetable). Its riders stay aboard.
- **A signal failure.** A stretch between two neighbouring stations, clear of both, runs at caution, 9 m/s (about 20 mph, `caution`), for about 12 minutes (`signalFor`): trains brake to it, crawl through and come out late. Amber lamps flash on posts at each end of the stretch. The faster timetable's new signals stop six in ten of them (`resignalHolds`), so the faster timetable's card says fewer signal failures.
- **Rail replacement buses.** Four minutes into a failure (`busAfter`) a bus pulls in at the stop outside the station behind the failed train, and another every two minutes up to three (`busGap`, `busFleet`). Each shuttles by road to the station ahead and back while the line's blocked: at each end it stands a minute (`busDwell`), sets down who it carried (those going further wait for the train on with their fare paid, as someone changing does, so they never give up) and takes up to 70 of the people waiting its way (`busSeats`), whose fare is taken as for the train. The stations either side are the nearest on the line, whether or not the train calls there, so there's always a road between them. A bus runs at 6 m/s along the road (`busSpeed`, town traffic and stops), slower than the train, so people whose patience runs out first still give up; once the line is clear the buses finish their trip and go. `trouble.bused` counts who they carried.
- **A spare unit** (a line upgrade, £8,000, offered once a train has failed): it stands in Stockport's carriage sidings, goes out while a failed train is being rescued, and halves the rescue, so the buses are seldom needed. On the map (`rescueFor` in `src/render/trains.ts`) it waits a tenth of the rescue for its crew, drives out of the sidings and along the line beside the blocked one (the fast line where a stopper failed, the branch's own track on the Hazel Grove line, where nothing else runs), crosses onto the blocked line for its last 150 m and buffers up to the failed unit's Stockport end (on the Marple line, where it would have to cross the city, it's drawn coming out from Piccadilly along the failed train's own track instead, a view-only shortcut) three quarters of the way through, then drives back to the sidings once the line is clear.

On the map: hazard lamps flash at both ends of a failed unit (`--map-hazard`), caution lamps on posts by a signal failure, and the buses (`src/render/buses.ts`, `--map-bus`, `--map-bus-band`), with "Rail Replacement" on boards at the front and sides, drive the roads between the stations' stops, the departure boards say Cancelled, Delayed or Bus replacement (Station life), (the kit part `busstop`: a bay, a kerb, a shelter and a flag) with people walking out to them and off them. Each bus drives in from the nearer bus depot (the network data's `depot` landmarks: Stagecoach's garages on Daw Bank in Stockport and on Hyde Road in Ardwick, drawn as a brick shed with buses in its yard) along the roads, timed to pull in at the stop as the sim starts it (quicker than in service, 14 m/s, and faster still where the run is long, so it sets off after the failure), and drives back there when the sim is done with it. Where a stop stands on a station's pad above the ground, its road comes down the pad's wall as a ramp no steeper than 1 in 10 rather than dropping off it (`terrain` check). The roads are `roads` in `src/data/network.json`, [lat, lon] from one station's way out to the next one's, drawn as a ribbon the houses keep off, laid again as the fine terrain comes in. Where one crosses a cutting between streets it's on a bridge, straight from one side's height to the other's, never down at rail level. `tools/osm-to-roads.mjs` draws each from OpenStreetMap's real streets (fetching them from the main API itself) and writes it into `roads`; where a kit draws the bridge the road crosses on, the road runs over the kit's deck and its `road` parts (Heaton Chapel's). The roads not yet redrawn are from memory (the data's `roadNote`). On the bot's line one or two things go wrong in 8 sim hours. At night or between the peaks they cost nobody; on a peak's shoulders a failed train costs a few dozen people, up to about 120 on the worst of seeds 1–12, mostly at Stockport, whose platform fills while its trains are held, and the buses carry a few dozen.

## Weather and seasons

The weather and the year are the sim's (`src/sim/weather.ts`, its numbers in `WEATHER` there), drawn from their own seeded random numbers (`weather.rngState` in the save, from the seed), so the same seed brings the same weather and the weather never moves anything else the game draws: with nobody feeling it (the bot's `--fair`) a game plays exactly as one without weather.

- **The year** turns a season every two game days (about 90 real minutes at 1×), spring, summer, autumn, winter, so the year is eight game days; a new game starts in early spring.
- **Spells**: sunny, cloudy, rain, fog and in winter snow, each lasting a few of the clock's hours (rain 1 to 3.5, fog 1.5 to 3, the rest 2 to 6), drawn by the season's odds (rain about a third of spells in autumn, a fifth in summer; snow about one in eight in winter). Rain or snow is always followed by a dry spell, so it rarely rains all day; fog only comes in between 03:00 and 11:00. The first game day stays dry (cloud and fog, no rain), so the opening is as it was.
- **What people feel**, through the door-to-door journey (Journeys, above), all gentle: in rain the walk feels 10% longer and an open platform's wait 15% longer, while under a canopy it feels as it does dry, so a canopy (and lifts, which shorten the walk) pays more on a wet day; snow makes the walk feel 40% longer and the wait 30% (10% under a canopy), slows the line to 85% of its speed and keeps 15% at home. In autumn leaves on the line take the line speed to 92% (96% with the faster timetable's new signals). Fog changes nothing but the view. The journeys are worked out again when a spell changes, so the reach's glow shrinks in the rain and comes back with the sun.
- **On the map** (`src/render/weather.ts`, view only): rain falls as streaks and snow as flakes in a box round where the camera looks, moved in their shaders so nothing is written per frame (a third of them on the low setting, none past whole-line zoom); a new spell fades in over a minute of real time at 1×. Under cover the clouds swell, grey and drift faster, the dome greys and the sun dims, its disc going in behind a thick sky; fog draws the view in to a few hundred metres and rain and snow a little; rain darkens the platforms (`--map-wet`) for a game hour or so after, and snow settles on the roofs, the platforms, the canopies and the ground over a game hour, melting over three after. Most people on the platforms put up a bright umbrella in the rain (`--map-brolly-*`), a few in the snow, never a cyclist. A belt of trees stands along the line beyond the outermost track, clear of the stations' kits: blossom among the fresh green in spring, deep green in summer, gold and rust in autumn, bare twigs in winter (`--map-leaf-*`, `--map-blossom`), and the ground's tint turns with them. The day's length follows the year (`solarHour`): sixteen hours of daylight at midsummer, eight at midwinter (so the evening peak is lamp-lit), the equinox early in spring as the real one is; the sun stands higher at a summer noon than a winter one.
- **Sound and settings**: rain hisses (filtered noise from the sound's bed) and patters on the canopies near where the camera looks; birds go quiet in rain and snow, and snow hushes the town. Fair weather in the settings draws every spell as a sunny or cloudy one, but only the look changes: the game's weather and what people feel are the same either way, so the same seed is the same game on every device (the option that changes the game less).
- **What it costs**: on seeds 1-3 over 8 sim hours it rains 0.5 to 1 sim hour (none on the first day) and the milestones hold; over 32 sim hours (a whole year) it's wet about a fifth of the time, and cash at the end is about 6% under the same seeds with the weather unfelt, passengers about 4%.

To look at a spell: `node tools/shots.mjs [hour ...] --weather <sun|cloud|rain|fog|snow>` (or with `--stations`) settles it over the map before each shot (`window.__weather`), and `--day <n>` starts a new game on that game day for its season (`window.__day`; snow wants a winter one); the files' names end `-<spell>`.

## Contract renewal

Running the line is an operating contract (`src/sim/contract.ts`), the first layer of the owner's prestige idea; eras and challenges (phase 11) build on the same shape later.

- **Reputation** is earned through the run (`reputation`): a point for every hundred passengers who started a journey, less three for each who gave up and went home and twenty for each departure more than five minutes late (`stats.late`); a cab taken instead counts half. So a well-run line banks more than a big one run badly. It's derived from the run's totals, so it keeps counting until the contract is renewed.
- **Renewing** is offered, never forced: once the player owns Piccadilly, the Network card (tap the cash pill) shows "Renew the contract" with the reputation it would bank, and the run plays on as long as they like. Renewing banks the reputation into the career, keeps the run's milestones (`owns:<station>`, `renewed`) and records the contract (its number, the day and what it banked); then everything else starts again from Stockport as a halt with a fresh seed: cash, stations, stages, homes grown, weather and trouble.
- **Perks** (`contract` in `src/data/network.json`): eleven in four branches (Capital, Works, Reach, Rolling stock), each branch bought in order and each perk dearer than the last in it, bought with reputation on the renewal sheet. They act through the sim's own numbers, read once when a run starts (`Sim.fx`, from the run's own `perks`): starting cash; a share off every price (stations, stages, upgrades and the line's, `Sim.cost`); a minute off the walk at every station (so journeys feel shorter, more people travel and the reach glows wider); the first extra unit standing in Stockport's sidings, free to put in service once platform 3 is built; the spare unit standing by from the start. With no perks every number is as it was, and the bot's `PLAY` is unchanged.
- **The career** (reputation unspent, perks, renewals, milestones, contracts) is saved apart from the run (`CAREER_VERSION`, Save below), so starting a run again never touches it.
- **The renewal sheet** (`src/ui/contract.ts`, kept plain for the redesign, and fetched when it's first opened) opens from the Network card's renew card: what this contract banks, the reputation to spend, and each branch's perks bought and the next one with its price (the rest stay hidden until they're next). Nothing changes until "Begin contract n"; closing the sheet leaves the run as it was. There's no recommended buy.
- **On the map:** beginning the next contract shows the whole line as every stage folds back down and each halt sinks and stands again in the fog (`syncStations`, in about a second, at once with reduced motion). A brass plaque on a sandstone pier by Stockport's Station Road entrance (the kit part `plaque`, `--map-brass`) reads the contract's number and grows a line for each contract renewed (its number, the day and what it banked); it stands once one has been. The unit in the sidings stands on the siding beside the spare's until it's put in service (its card says Free). The wider reach shows in the glow, as journeys feel shorter, and quicker building as the stages rising sooner in the run.
- **Start over** starts the run again with the career's perks; once there's a career, its first tap also offers "Career too", which wipes the career as well (`wireStartOver` in `src/ui/saves.ts`). The default, tapping Start over again, keeps it.
- **Pacing** (`tools/bot.mjs --runs 2`, the `contract` entry in `tools/baseline.json`): a first run renewed as soon as Piccadilly is bought banks about 62 and buys Framework contractor (15% off), Signposting (a minute off the walk) and A unit in the sidings; the second run reaches Piccadilly about a quarter sooner (4.3 sim hours against 5.9). Renewing as soon as it can banks less each time, as quicker runs carry fewer people, so it levels off: five contracts in a row reach Piccadilly at 3.6 h.

## Numbers that matter (`TUNING` in `src/sim/sim.ts`)

- Time: a sim second is a real-speed second (speeds, dwells and patience are real), and the game's 1× runs three of them a real second (`GAME_PER_REAL`), so 1×, 3× and 10× are 3, 9 and 30 times real. The clock runs faster than sim seconds, and not at one rate (`src/sim/day.ts`, the owner's note that the day was long and the night pointless to wait through): 6 clock seconds a sim second through the peaks (06:00–09:30 and 16:00–19:00 at full, where the trains and the crowds play off each other), 12 between them and 60 through the night (23:00–05:00), eased over about an hour at each change so the sky and the clock never jump. A game day is about 8,150 sim seconds, 45 real minutes at 1× (15 at 3×, 4.5 at 10×): the peaks about 21 real minutes, the hours between 22, the night about two. `day.ts` maps sim time to the clock both ways (`clockAt`, `simAt`, `simAtHour`), so it can say when an hour comes; everything that reads the hour or counts days asks it. What the clock paces steps by the clock rather than the sim: demand (each clock hour carries the trips it did when the whole day ran at 6×, so a game day earns about what it did, 2.5% less on the bot's line, in about half the real time), homes growing, the weather's spells and the year, and the gaps between things going wrong. Trains, dwells, patience and platform caps stay in real-speed sim seconds, so between the peaks fewer trains run a clock hour and each carries more. The top bar's clock shows » while the night races by.
- Fare: `(1.5 + 0.6 × miles) × fareMult`. `fareMult` (13.5) is the single knob for overall pacing; it was six times what it was when the clock ran at 6× (12), so a sixth of the trains earn the same money a real minute, and rose to 13.5 when homes and draw came from the census.
- Seats: 75 per car, 2 cars to start. Boarding: 2 doors a car, 0.7 passengers a second through each, slowing as the train fills, all through the stop, so a 2-car unit takes about 110 in a 45 s stop and longer trains board faster as well as holding more. Passengers get off the moment the train arrives.
- Speeds are real (33.5 m/s = 75 mph) on a true-scale line. Stockport to Piccadilly takes about ten sim minutes plus stops (just over three real minutes at 1×); the timetable plans 45 s at a stop and 120 s to turn round at the termini (Timetable, above).
- Journeys and patience: see Journeys above (`walk`, `walkRound`, `walkStairs`, `liftsSave`, the weights, `delayWeight`, `budget`, `budgetSoft`, `demand`). Platform cap 150, 320 extended.
- 82% of fares collected at a halt, 90% with a booking office, all with barriers.
- Catchment: +20% for the rest of a big station; the way across and lifts save minutes instead.
- Things going wrong: none in the first six clock hours, then one every 15 clock hours or so, by day; a failed train rescued in about 9 minutes (4 with a spare unit), buses from 4 minutes in; a signal failure 12 minutes at 20 mph, six in ten stopped by the faster timetable (Things going wrong, above).
- Taxi ranks: six cabs, 60% of those giving up take one while one's in, four to a cab, away 3 min plus 4 min a mile there and back, 1.5 times the train fare to the player (Taxi ranks, above).

The bot (`tools/bot.mjs`) measures pacing on seeds 1–3 against `tools/baseline.json`; the `balance` playbook has the table. Current pacing at 1× (three sim seconds a real second): an extra unit in about 8 real minutes, Heaton Chapel in about 13 (the bot's run; a player buying nothing first takes longer), Levenshulme at about 39 minutes (13 at 3×), Ardwick at about an hour and five minutes, Piccadilly at about two hours (40 minutes at 3×). The peaks press on the bought stations too: over the first two game days, of the bot's 290 or so who give up (240 lost, 50 by cab), about a third give up beyond Stockport, mostly at Heaton Chapel and Levenshulme in the second morning, where a halt's one train an eight- or nine-minute gap uses up the budgets of people whose trip only just fits. Extra units relieve it most (about half the second morning's lost), canopies and the faster timetable a little, longer trains once trains run full (the first morning); lifts and second platforms bring more people than they keep, so Stockport's platform fills and the extension is the next fix. `test/sim.test.ts` pins the Heaton Chapel pacing so tuning changes are deliberate.

## Station kits

A kit is JSON with `faces` (where each direction's passengers wait: a line near the platform edge and a `depth` back into the platform, 4 m if not given), an optional `entrance` (the `u` passengers come and go at, for a terminus whose way in is its concourse rather than a subway or footbridge) and `parts`. Part types in `src/render/stations.ts`: `platform`, `canopy`, `lamps`, `box`, `windows`, `shelter` (with `back` saying which side its glass is on, and `vault` for a barrel roof over blue panels), `footbridge`, `lattice` (an open plate-girder footbridge with lattice parapets on trestles, Ardwick's) with its `flights` (an open stair in two flights along the line down to a platform), `lifts`, `retail`, `barriers`, `signalbox`, `shed`, `footprint` (a building from its real outline; `floors` bands it into storeys of ribbon glazing, as Gateway House), `subway` (a passage under the line with a stair head on each platform), `rails` (a track no service runs on: sidings, bays, platform lines, through `(u, v)` points, with an optional buffer stop), `board` (a departure board for trains `north`, `south` or `both` ways, on posts over a platform or on a wall), `carpark` (bays that fill, on tarmac or on a `deck` someone else's building carries), `cycles` (a row of `n` Sheffield stands) and `taxi` (a rank of `n` cab spaces from its front at `(u, v)` back along `back`, its kerb and shelter on the `kerb` side, people coming from `door`) and `busstop` (where a rail replacement bus stands at `(u, v)` along `back`, its shelter and flag on the `kerb` side, people coming from `door`; on the halt, since the buses serve any station), `hipped` (a hipped roof over a building's footprint, eaves at `y0`, ridge `rise` above them: Davenport's, Hazel Grove's and Heaton Chapel's booking offices), and `recesses` (a row of `n` arched recesses in an embankment's wall at `v`, at street level: Levenshulme's). `retail` is a shop that opens and shuts (Station life, below). A part with no gate is the station's halt (fogged while the station is locked). A part may carry `upgrade: "<id>"` (or a list, all needed: a canopy over the second platform is `["canopy", "platform2"]`) and then only shows once bought, and `until: "<id>"` to go again when that's bought (Stockport's halt canopy, replaced by the island's). `scenery: true` marks what isn't the player's (roads, bridges, walls, sidings, signal boxes, depots) and always shows, never fogged. `arches` (a brick bridge over the line with segmental arches cut through it, piers to the ground between them and nothing solid below an arch's springing, Heaton Chapel's road bridge) `ramp` (a sloping path along the line with handrails) and `drum` (a glass entrance round in plan under a flat slab, Stockport's on Station Road). `stairs` is a footbridge's stair tower on its own, for a platform built after the bridge. `road` is a street from OpenStreetMap through `(u, v)` points, `w` wide, laid on the ground under each point (or at a point's own height, `[u, v, y]`, over a bridge's deck or abutment) and seated again as the terrain comes in finer: it joins a kit's bridge decks, ranks and stops to the real streets painted on the ground, and never clears the station's pad. Everything bought must show: put a part on the halt's side unless it really needs a later stage too. Add a new part type by adding its shape to the `Part` union and a builder to `partBuilders`, both in that file. The saved game's shape is `GameState` in `src/sim/types.ts`.

Frame: `u` along the corridor towards Manchester, `v` across it (positive `v` is east at Stockport: the main building, Grand Central and Stockport Exchange), `y` up. Lengths along the line and across it are real (until 13 October 2026 everything across was drawn 1.25 times real, and the trains 1.3 times real width; the owner had it taken out so the stations line up with the real buildings and ground round them). The trains (2.8 m wide and 4 m to the roof), buses, cars and taxis are drawn at their real size, and so are the people, the canopies, the platform lamps and the signals (the owner, 13 October 2026; only the departure boards stay bigger than life, so they read on a phone). The trains pass clear of each other and of the platforms, whose edges stand 1.68 m from the track. What stands round a station is held to the real place by the `surroundings` check (no browser): every taxi rank, car park, bus stop and bus link within 1.5 m of the real ground under it (the LIDAR less the rail's level) and touching a street (a real one painted on the ground, a kit's `road` or a bridge's deck; a car park within 25 m of one), none on a real building, both ends of every kit `road` meeting a street, a deck or the map's edge, and the replacement buses' roads crossing the railway only on a bridge, never running along it. Its `LATER` list names the stations still to put right (`docs/audits/station-surroundings.md`). Platform lengths, buildings, footbridges and subways come from an OpenStreetMap export: `tools/osm-to-kit.mjs` drafts a kit's parts from one, and each kit's `note` says what it took and what was fitted by hand.

Where the tracks are: each track in `src/data/network.json` has an open-line `offset` (0, 3.52, 7.36 and 11.04 m, west to east: down slow, down fast, up fast, up slow), and the `layout` list moves them around a station (`station` with `u0`–`u1` in its frame) or along a stretch (`s0`–`s1`), from the real tracks in the export. Tracks ease between layouts over `ease` metres, and a train turns with its track. A kit's platforms stand 1.68 m from the track they serve, all the way along: side platforms outside the slow lines, Stockport's and Piccadilly's islands between tracks. The stopper turns round at both ends, so at Stockport (platform 4) and Piccadilly (platform 5) both its tracks run into one platform and a train never jumps across on reversing. A station's `stop` moves where trains stop along the line without moving its kit, so a unit stands in the middle of its platforms: Piccadilly's trains stop 35 m short so the longest clears the buffers, Heaton Chapel's 16 m north, Ardwick's 8 m north, Davenport's and Woodsmoor's 20 m south, Middlewood's 10 m and Disley's 12 m north, and New Mills Newtown's 24 m south, against Albion Road's bridge at its north end, so its extension is at the south end. Every unit the line runs stands wholly alongside the platform extended, the two-car one on the platform as it is; where the real platforms are too short for six cars, the kit's `longest` says how many they take (Woodsmoor's four) and a longer unit stands off the end, as at a real short platform.

The corridor is the real line, resampled every 60 m, so it bends a little at every point, while a kit is straight. So the map draws the line straight along a station's frame through its `straight` stretch (`[u0, u1]` in the network data: its platforms, extensions and the bridges by them) and eases back to the corridor over 150 m either side (`place` in `src/sim/network.ts`, which the tracks, wires, masts, signals and trains all draw from); the corridor's direction turns smoothly through each point, so a track drawn well off it runs on without a step. Only the drawing: the sim measures along the corridor, so distances and times are the corridor's.

The Buxton line's stations (Middlewood, Disley, New Mills Newtown) are kits like the rest, from OpenStreetMap's main API and the photo library, with the road and path bridges over them as scenery: the Middlewood Way's bridge across Middlewood at an angle (`footprint` parts), the A6's arch at Disley and Albion Road's bridge at New Mills Newtown. Middlewood has no road: its stages are the second platform and the steps to the Way, and the replacement buses stop at the end of the nearest lane. Each kit has to be listed as well as the data: the opening's (the line from Edgeley Junction to Piccadilly) in `src/render/kits.ts`, which come with the page, and every line out's in `src/render/kitsOut.ts`, fetched just after the first frame. Until then a line out's stations stand as empty groups; `fillStations` builds them into those groups as their kits come, and the layers that read a kit (passengers, taxis, bus links, the buses' stops, Strain) take them in (the `kits` check holds each kit to its list).

The `geometry` check group (`tools/checks/geometry.mjs`, no browser) measures it from the data and the renderer's own shapes: the platform edge 1.68 m (within 0.1) from its track at points all along it; no solid part (anything between rail top and roof height but rails) in the space a train sweeps on any track a service runs on, expresses included, and running in to and out of every platform each service uses; each unit standing wholly alongside every platform its service uses, 1.68 m from its edge, centred where the sim stops it, short of the buffers; and each car's bogies on the network's track, or standing, on its platform's track or rails. Run it with `node tools/check.mjs geometry`; `node tools/shots.mjs --stations` shoots every station grown, from above and at platform level, to hold against its note and photos; a view after the ids, `name=du,dv,r,turn,tilt`, shoots that one instead (`bridge=-77,2,40,0,1.45` looks along the line at Heaton Chapel's bridge).

## The network

The line is a graph (`src/sim/network.ts`, `docs/decisions/ADR-2026-10-13-network-as-a-graph.md`). Corridors are real lines' centrelines, each drawn towards Manchester and joined at named junctions (`junctions` in the network data): today the Hazel Grove line runs from beyond New Mills Newtown on the Buxton line (phase 5 step 2) up to Edgeley Junction, half a mile south of Stockport, and the line to Piccadilly runs on from it. The Marple line (phase 5 step 3, `marple`) runs from beyond Marple through Romiley, Reddish North and Ashburys to Ardwick Junction, west of Ardwick station where OpenStreetMap has it, and the Rose Hill branch (`rose-hill`, a single line) from beyond Rose Hill to Marple Wharf Junction, a kilometre south of Romiley; both are lines off the spine, with their own ground. The Crewe line (phase 5 step 3, `crewe`, `docs/briefs/crewe-line.md`) runs from beyond Wilmslow through Handforth and Cheadle Hulme to Edgeley Junction, coming in from the south-south-west and spreading its two tracks to meet the slow lines there, and its Macclesfield leg (`macclesfield`) from beyond Poynton through Bramhall to Cheadle Hulme North Junction, just north of Cheadle Hulme station, where OpenStreetMap has it; Wilmslow South Junction is named for the Styal line, which joins there. Both are wired throughout and have their own ground; their stations and service come next. The Marple lines run on from Ardwick Junction as the real Hope Valley lines do, on their own pair beside the Longsight lines on their east side (`hope-valley-north` and `hope-valley-south`, tracks of the line to Piccadilly that start at the junction: a track's `start` or `end` can name a junction), into Piccadilly's platforms 1 to 4, so a Marple train and a stopper or an express never hold each other there (14 October 2026). The other junctions are named points with no line beyond them yet, where phase 5's lines will join: Heaton Norris (Reddish South and Stalybridge), Slade Lane (the Styal line), and Edgeley again for the Crewe line. Distances along the line are chainage: it runs from 0 at the start of the spine (`spine`: the corridors the ground, trees, masts and signals are laid along, end to end, which the view reads as `net.spine`; each corridor off it is a line of its own, `net.lines` the spine and those, the ground and what stands along the line laid along each) and on through every junction, so a distance and its corridor name one place and every route's distances grow towards Manchester. Tracks belong to a corridor; a service's `tracks` list its pair leg by leg (`Route` in `src/sim/network.ts`), and its trains pass from one leg's pair to the next's at the junction. A train's place is its route's leg (`Train.leg`, so its corridor and track) and its chainage there; the platforms, holds, timetable, trouble and expresses all measure along the route, which is chainage, so they work through a junction as on one line. A signal failure's stretch lies between two stations on a route and names the corridors it runs along (`Caution.on`), so trains on another line at the same chainage aren't slowed. The four main lines begin at Edgeley Junction, where the expresses come on.

### Where the tracks run

A layout has to make sense as a railway, not only carry the trains (the owner, 13 October 2026, on tracks tangled across Stockport Viaduct: "make sure we have good enough distances between stations and sections so we don't end up with this spaghetti junction"). `src/sim/layout.ts` checks the rule from the data, and `test/layout.test.ts` runs it in `npm test`, naming each place that breaks it; `LAYOUT` in `src/sim/network.ts` holds its figures:

- **No sharp shifts.** No track, bay's rails or kit's rails shifts sideways faster than 1 in 6 (across per along): a real 1 in 9 turnout's diverging road leaves at about that, and a throat on a curve, like Stockport's north end in OpenStreetMap, fans a little faster (up to about 1 in 6.3).
- **No crossings.** Two tracks on a corridor keep their order: they run at least 3.3 m apart (real track centres are about 3.4 to 4 m, and the trains, 2.8 m wide, pass half a metre apart), or together within 0.24 m (one road worked both ways, like the stopper's into the platform it turns in), and between the two only while merging, parting or crossing over, for at most 120 m. A station's rails into a bay leave a track at a turnout and cross no other. A train that has to change sides crosses over beyond the throat (a stopper turning in Stockport's platform 4 or 3 comes in on the up slow), on a crossover drawn with its rails (Crossovers, below); the tracks don't. A station's rails, its kit's and its bays', are drawn along the line as the tracks are (`alongLine` in `src/render/stations.ts`), so their `v` is an offset across the line even where it bends beyond the station's straight stretch.
- **Room between sections.** Each stretch where tracks move (a station's throat, a crossover) and each junction gets clear line either side: 100 m at least before the next.
- **On the deck.** On the viaduct and the arches every track is on the deck: the deck is drawn as wide as the tracks on it are (`deck`), with 4.8 m of cess beyond the outer ones, so a station's own rails must join the tracks before a structure or stay within them there.
- **Blocks.** No block section is shorter than the longest train and its overlap (262 m), and no signal stands where tracks move (`signals` and `blocks` keep both).

**Stockport**, re-laid to it from OpenStreetMap (13 October 2026): west to east the down slow at platform 4 (v 0.2), the down fast at 3 (19.2) and the up fast at 2 (24) between the two islands, the up slow at 1 (38.56) with the Hazel Grove line, and platform 0's bay (42.08), narrowed from the islands' full width towards the real one at the platforms' north end so the throat fits. The platforms end at u = 120, and each track fans in to its place on the viaduct by u = 280 to 390 at no more than 1 in 6, on the viaduct's first spans as on the real one; bay 3a and platform 0 join the down fast and the up slow in the throat, the Edgeley lines and the carriage sidings join the down slow before the viaduct, and the sidings leave their lead at turnouts. The Hazel Grove line runs east of the main lines from Edgeley Junction and merges into platform 1 over the last 70 m before it.

**Manchester Piccadilly**, re-laid to it from OpenStreetMap (14 October 2026): the four Longsight lines (from Stockport) run into platform 5 and the bays 6 to 12, as the real ones run into 5 to 12, and the two Hope Valley lines (the Marple and Rose Hill trains') into 1 to 4. The Longsight lines close up into one road with the down slow (v 0, platform 5, the stopper's and the halt's) from u = -880 to -775, beside where the bays' lead leaves it (a game choice: the real throat is a ladder of crossovers), so a stopper leaves 5 on the road it came in on. The Hope Valley line towards Manchester holds its 14.88 into platform 3 and the line out eases from 18.4 to platform 2's 23.68 through the throat from u = -580 to -360, clear of the islands; platform 4 leaves the first westwards and platform 1 the second eastwards. Bays 6 to 12 leave the down slow westwards in one lead from u = -880, on the arches as the real throat begins there, each peeling off it at 1 in 6.5 to its platform's line; platforms 13 and 14's scenery tracks run on along the lead beyond them. Bay 1 leaves the up slow eastwards at the platforms' end, and a bay's rails beyond its far end are the track they join. A train for a platform off its own track crosses over beyond the throat: the expresses from the fast lines into 6 and 7 and out again, beyond where the bays' lead leaves (a platform's `cross` holds its crossovers out to there), and the Marple trains between their pair and 1, 2 and 4, or, before Piccadilly's platforms stage, over to 5 from u = -400.

**Crossovers.** A train never jumps across: where its platform isn't on its own track it crosses over to it beyond the throat, where the two come closest, nearest the station clear of the longest unit standing there (an express clear of its own length) or no nearer than the platform's `cross`, for one reached across the throat's other roads, over a crossover 12 times as long as they're apart (`crossing` in `src/render/trains.ts`). The map draws each with its rails, from the same description the trains ride (`crossovers`), at every station where a service calls or turns round: a ladder across the tracks between where it crosses several, like the stopper's from the up slow to Stockport's platform 4 north of the throat, and a plain crossover between a line's pair where it turns at the end of its run. The `geometry` check holds every unit between tracks to a drawn crossover, and no crossover shifts faster than 1 in 6.

The Buxton line and the lines out build to it: their stations and junctions leave the room it asks for. Places still to re-lay are listed in the test, each coming off the list once it keeps the rule.

## The line between stations

The line is made of sections (`docs/briefs/line-between-stations.md`, phase 5 step 11): the line between two neighbouring stations, or a station and a junction, along one corridor, places of their own (`src/sim/places.ts`) that come with the stations at both their ends. Each section's real line speed and the share of it under wires are in the network data's `sections`, each corridor's in chainage order, read from OpenStreetMap's `maxspeed` and `electrified` on the running lines by `tools/osm-to-sections.mjs` (run it again for a new line out).

- **Line speed by section** (`Sim.sectionAt`, `Sim.runSpeed`, `SECTION_SPEED`). A section starts at the game's 75 mph on the stopper's own line, so the opening plays as it did, and at its real speed where that's lower elsewhere (the Buxton line 45 to 60 mph, the Marple line 40 to 60). Once it's the player's, its card offers its line speed 15 mph a step up to 90, at £3,300 a mile a step (the old faster timetable's £20,000 over the stopper's six miles). Trains run each run between two stops at the speed that takes them through it in the time its sections' own speeds would (the game's 75 beyond any section), and the timetable and the journeys plan it the same, so a faster section is minutes off every ride through it. Once every section of the stopper's line is raised the line has the faster timetable's recovery and resistance to leaves (`line.timetable`, derived) and its 90 boards; its new signals are each section's resignalling now.
- **Resignalling by section** (`SECTION_SIGNALS`, `Sim.sites`, `Sim.blocks`, `Network.closer`). Once a section is the player's its card offers resignalling, one stage at £5,000 a mile. Its signal sites are re-laid closer: each run of the network's sites through it (a run breaks where a throat skipped one) is shared out evenly into as many blocks as fit no shorter than a train and its overlap (262 m), so the run's ends stay put, and the sim's blocks follow (Heaton Chapel – Levenshulme's four sites become six, Middlewood – Hazel Grove's seven eleven; a section with one site or two 440 m apart keeps them). Trains there keep 260 m behind the one ahead (`separationClose`, middle to middle) rather than 400, so the closer signals are what holds them apart. A signal failure whose stretch is in a resignalled section is held off six times in ten (`resignalHolds`), as the faster timetable's new signals were. On the map (`src/render/lineside.ts`) its colour-light signals stand at its sites, showing what the blocks beyond hold (Signalling, below), with a location case at each end opposite its speed board; choosing the card ghosts them and frames the section, buying makes them rise into place. Before any is resignalled the line has no colour-light signals drawn. The bot resignals once the stopper's line is all raised and it has £60,000 to spare, the stopper's line first (at about 6.2 game hours), then the cheapest it owns.
- **Electrification by section** (`SECTION_WIRES`, `Sim.runAccel`). A section starts wired where most of it is under wires on the real line (the network data's `sections[...].wired`): the main line, the Buxton line to Hazel Grove and the Marple line's last stretch into Ardwick Junction. Elsewhere (the Buxton line beyond Hazel Grove, the Marple line from Ashburys out, Rose Hill) its trains run as diesels, accelerating at `accelDiesel` (0.7 m/s²) rather than an electric unit's `accel` (1.0); a run between two stops accelerates by the share of it under wires, and the timetable and the journeys plan it the same. Once the section is the player's its card offers electrification, one stage at £8,000 a mile, and its trains accelerate as electric units through it. Its card's figures say Electric or Diesel. On the map (`src/render/sections.ts`) an electrified section shows masts outside its outer tracks every 55 m with an arm over them, on the wired lines' grid, and a contact wire over each track (`--map-wire`), only where the line had none; its card ghosts them, buying makes them rise into place. The bot electrifies a section it owns once it has £60,000 to spare, the cheapest first (the Marple line's from about 13 game hours). The opening is untouched: at 8 hours `PLAY` is as it was. The fleet (phase 5 step 5) will choose diesel or electric units; until then wires are about the line.
- **Flying junctions** (`JUNCTION_FLYOVER`, `Sim.flown`, `Network.shares`). The sim counts the seconds trains stand at a junction's signal held for another train's way through it (`held` in the save, by junction, the whole run). A junction comes with any section through it; once trains have stood there 10 minutes in all its card offers a flying junction, £60,000: ways through it that cross no longer wait on each other, while ways onto the same track still take turns. Its card's figures are Trains held (minutes) and Junction (Flat or Flying). Today's network seldom holds a train at a junction (the bot's games: none in 16 game hours, now the Marple trains have their own pair past Ardwick; Marple Wharf, where the Rose Hill line crosses the Marple line on the flat, is the one place left where ways cross), so none is offered yet; it waits for the late game's shared lines (phase 5). On the map (`src/render/sections.ts`) any junction's flying junction is a flyover: a concrete deck leaving the line beyond its outer tracks on one side, climbing over them at the junction on piers to 7.5 m and coming down beyond them on the other, rising into place when bought; its card ghosts it. The trains' own tracks stay drawn at grade, under it: laying the flyover's own tracks in the sim (and its layout) is handed on. The bot buys one wherever it's offered once it has £80,000.
- **On the map** (`src/render/sections.ts`, fetched just after the start): a raised section shows its renewed track, pale fresh ballast (`--map-ballast-new`) under its tracks, and a line-speed board at each end reading its speed. Its step's card ghosts them and frames the section; its Built chip frames it. A tap on the line between two stations (where the picker finds no station or train) opens its section's card, as does Next to on the stations at its ends. The card's figures are its line speed and length.
- **The bot** buys a step once it has £45,000 to spare, on the stopper's line first, then the cheapest anywhere it owns; it has the stopper's line at 90 at about 6 game hours (the faster timetable was at about 4), and towards Hazel Grove and along the Marple line as it reaches them.

Slow sections and held junctions show in Strain (below). Handed on from this step: the flyover's own tracks in the sim and its layout, and junctions that hold trains often enough to offer one (the late game's shared lines).

## Signalling

Every track is cut into block sections (`blocks` in `src/sim/network.ts`, and `Sim.blocks` as resignalling has them): its boundaries are the signal sites along its corridor (one every 440 m of chainage, none within 280 m of a station) and its own ends (a junction, a buffer stop, the edge of the map). A block with a station in it is the station's own, worked by its platforms (Platforms, above); the rest are open line. Once a step, before the trains move, the sim marks the blocks each train's length covers on each track it spans (`occupy`), and a running train doesn't pass a signal into an open block another train is in: it stands 10 m short (`signalStand`) until the block clears (`signalRoom`). The 400 m a train keeps behind the one ahead on its line still holds, inside a station's block too. At a junction a train's way through is set for it as it comes within 700 m (`claim`): from one leg's track onto the next's where its route changes corridor, or straight on along its track past a junction on its own corridor (`movements`). Two different ways through a junction conflict if they share a track or cross (`conflicts`), and a train whose way conflicts with one held by another train, across the junction or nearer it coming up, waits at the junction's signal: the first to come up goes first. Today's line seldom brings trains close, so it bites behind a failed train, where the trains behind now stand at the signal before its block rather than 400 m behind it, and at Edgeley Junction once the Crewe line joins; it's the late-game pressure of phase 5's shared lines. A resignalled section's sites are re-laid closer and its blocks with them (The line between stations, above). Where a section is resignalled, each signal shows what the sim says for the lines it faces (`Sim.aspect`, refreshed every half second): red where the block beyond holds a train, yellow where the one after does or a station's block beyond has a train in it, green when both are clear (`--map-signal-red`, `--map-signal-yellow`, `--map-signal-green`). The `signals` check group drives a failed train into a block and reads the lamps. On seeds 1-3 the bot's game is unchanged.

## Trains

Passenger services are in `services` in `src/data/network.json`, each with a `route` of stations south to north. The main-line stopper runs its whole route, Stockport to Piccadilly, calling at every owned station and always at both ends; its units are the ones the line upgrades buy. The Hazel Grove line has a `home` (Stockport): it runs from there only as far down its route as stations are owned, with one unit to start (Frequency, below), from when Davenport is bought, and buying Woodsmoor or Hazel Grove extends it. It turns round in Stockport's platform 1, so a journey between the branch and the main line changes at Stockport: at the end of a train's run everyone still aboard gets off, counted as `changed`, and joins the platform for the train that goes their way with their fare already paid (it was taken where the journey began). The real Buxton-line trains run through to Manchester; until the player buys that (Through to Manchester, below) they turn at Stockport, a game choice, so changing trains is something to watch. Where different services go each way from a station (Stockport, once the branch is open), its passengers split between the two ways by how many people live each way as well as by the hour, so a short branch doesn't take half of Stockport; elsewhere the split is the hour's alone. Trains are drawn in their service's livery on its tracks, each car its own body riding on two bogies on the track (`unitPoses` in `src/render/trains.ts`), so a unit follows curves and crossovers rather than cutting across them. Near a station it's drawn at its platform (below).

The branch stations come after Piccadilly in the buying order, so the incremental loop goes on past the city.

**The Marple line** (`marple` and `rose-hill` in `services`): two services out from Piccadilly with a `home` there, like the Hazel Grove line's from Stockport. The Marple trains run from when Ashburys is bought, one unit, as far out as stations are owned towards Marple, calling at every owned one and turning at the furthest; the Rose Hill trains run only once Rose Hill is bought (`opens`), one unit, adding a train along the shared stretch to Romiley. Both run on the Marple line's own tracks to Ardwick Junction and on their own pair beside the Longsight lines into Piccadilly, as the real Hope Valley lines do, where they turn in platforms 1 to 4 on the north side once Piccadilly's platforms are built, and before that in the stopper's 5 (a game choice), so a Marple train and a stopper can wait on each other there (the platforms stage is the fix). Prices go on from Hazel Grove's beside the Buxton line's: Ashburys 300,000, Belle Vue 340,000, Ryder Brow 380,000, Reddish North 430,000, Brinnington 480,000, Bredbury 540,000, Romiley 600,000, Rose Hill 650,000 and Marple 680,000. Census homes and draw as for the rest (Homes and draw), except that a line off the spine only takes the drivers and bus riders no station on the spine would, so today's pools stay as they were. A journey between the Marple line and the main line changes at Piccadilly.

A train is drawn at the platform the sim gave it (`berthsOf`, `offsetOn` and `unitPoses` in `src/render/trains.ts`): running in, it crosses from its own track to that platform's track (or the rails drawn into a bay) where the two come closest, nearest the station and clear of the longest unit standing there, over a crossover 12 times as long as the tracks are apart (60 m at least), drawn with its rails (Where the tracks run, Crossovers); leaving, it crosses back the same way. A train turning round leaves on the track it came in on, so it never jumps across. The geometry check sweeps every service in and out of every platform it uses at every station.

## Frequency

Every local service's timetable is something to buy on its card (`frequency` on the services in `src/data/network.json`, `Sim.unitsFor`, `unitsAllDay`, `unitsPeak`; docs/briefs/services.md, part b). A service starts with one unit; its steps are a second unit, then the real off-peak timetable, then the real peak one, each with a note of where its numbers come from. The real frequencies are scaled the same way for every line: a real train an hour is one and a half units here, as the stopper's three units are its real two an hour (a game choice: the clock runs six to sixty times faster than the trains, so the real frequency on the clock would be a train every few minutes). So the stopper and the Buxton and Marple lines each go to two units, three all day, and one more in the peaks; the Rose Hill line, three trains every two hours, to two all day and one more in the peaks. The stopper's steps cost what its extra units did (£6,000, £20,000, £50,000) and still wait on Stockport's platform 3; the Hazel Grove line's £15,000, £40,000 and £70,000, the Marple line's £20,000, £50,000 and £80,000, Rose Hill's £30,000 and £60,000, offered once each runs.

**Peak units.** A peak timetable's units run in the peaks (06:30 to 09:30 and 16:00 to 19:00 by the clock, `TUNING.peaks`): a peak unit leaves the end where it rests (`stable`: Stockport for the stopper and the Hazel Grove line, Piccadilly for the Marple lines) from the start of each peak, and once a round trip brings it back after the peak's end it takes nobody on, stands its turnaround and goes off its platform to rest (`Sim.resting`, `unrest`; `state: 'away'`, holding no platform and in no block). It comes back to a free platform a turnaround before the next peak, keeping the gap behind the last unit to leave there. It stays on the map while it rests: in Stockport's carriage sidings, or on the roads through Piccadilly's platforms 13 and 14 (`roads`, a game choice while those aren't in service). The gap the timetable keeps between units counts the peak units only in the peaks, and the journeys plan on the all-day ones, so a peak timetable carries the peaks' crowds rather than shortening the wait people plan for. Every unit a step buys runs, so it shows; choosing a branch's step frames one of its units.

The bot buys a branch's next step once it runs to three stations or more and there's three times its price, the cheapest first: the Hazel Grove line's second unit at about 8 sim hours and the Marple line's at about 13.

## Through to Manchester

Its route changes track there, from the branch's into platform 1 to the slow lines on (`switches`: two legs of one corridor meeting at a station), so a train for Manchester crosses over to the down slow north of the throat and one from Manchester comes in on the up slow. It turns at Piccadilly in 12 or 11, where the real Buxton line's go. The bot buys it once it's offered and there's twice its price, and builds Piccadilly's shed and bays for it once there's twice both: at about 10.5 sim hours. ## Expresses

Avanti West Coast and CrossCountry run through from beyond the map (`through` on their services in the network data, `src/sim/expresses.ts`). Each comes on where the fast lines begin south of Stockport by the clock (`through` in the network data, `nextSlot`): one of each every two clock hours through the real day, Avanti's from 06:30 to 22:30 and CrossCountry's from 07:30 to 21:30, so the first is about 06:00 and the last about 23:00 as on the real timetable and the night is quiet. Each service runs two units taking its slots by turns (`EXPRESS_UNITS`), since between the peaks, where the clock runs fast, a round trip off the map takes longer than two clock hours. It runs at 40 m/s (`expressSpeed`), calls at Stockport where the player has built its platform that way (3 north with the second-platform stage, 2 south with platforms 0, 1 and 2) and otherwise runs through, turns round at Piccadilly whether or not the player owns it (its front where the longest stopping unit's would be, so it clears the buffers), and goes back off the map to London Euston or Bristol Temple Meads, where it's hidden (`state: 'away'`) until its next slot. None come on through the night, so the engineering train has the fast lines to itself. At a stop an express takes a platform like any train, so it can hold a stopper. At a station the player owns each call pays an access charge (£40, `accessCharge`) and its long-distance passengers, 30 a call at the morning peak's height and fewer by the demand curve (`expressPax`), off and as many on, each pay £2 of their fare to the station (`throughShare`): `StationState.through` and `stats.through` count them, `stats.express` what the expresses paid. They carry none of the line's own passengers (a game choice: they're not in the journeys) and never fail (the player has nothing to rescue them with). On the bot's line they call at Stockport from the first few minutes, about 26 times a game day at the stations the bot owns, and earn about £2,400 a game day (about £8,000 in 8 sim hours; about £2,900 over the first two game days, against £3,000 before shorter days).

## Pressure on the map

The view shows the sim's pressure instead of text (`src/render/passengers.ts`, `src/render/trains.ts`), all of it cosmetic. People board at the platform their train stands at (`Train.pf`): when a stopper stands at another platform than the face they wait on (Stockport's 3 rather than 4, Piccadilly's bays), they hurry by the stairs, or along a terminus's concourse, to its edge, and those getting off step out onto it and leave the same way; nobody walks to an express. When a station's lost count rises, whoever gave up waiting does a little shrug (a hop with the shoulders up) before walking out. People turned away by a full platform show too, a few at a time and never a crowd: they come in at the stairs on the busier side, shrug and go back. A train's windows go from cool glass when it's empty (`--map-win-empty`) to warm (`--map-win-busy`) as it fills. A train running a minute or more late carries an amber lamp (`--map-late`) on the roof over its leading cab, growing until it's five minutes late; on time it looks as it always has. Riders appear in the windows, ten seats to a side per car, and once it's 85% full people stand in the doorways. A gold coin (`--map-coin`) turns just above the name of the station earning most as the camera sees it, at any tilt (lifted along the camera's own up by a share of its distance, never below 24 m): the owned station with the highest takings over roughly the last game hour and a half. It only moves when another station beats it by a tenth, and it doesn't show while only one station is owned.

### Strain

The problems layer (`docs/design/buying.md`, section 6): a map view, **Strain**, beside Line, Mins and Light, off at the start and remembered on the device (`loadViews` in `src/app/prefs.ts`, under its own key so the settings sheet never writes over it). `src/ui/strain.ts` reads the sim and never changes it: a platform from 60% of its cap (`Sim.capacity`, waiting on each face), people giving up this clock hour (from the sim's running `lost`, counted by the hour in the view), late departures (through `Sim.onDepart`, the worst for each service, station and way this clock hour, a minute late or more; expresses aside) and car parks once every space is taken. It's fetched just after the start, and gives each card a line of plain figures at its top ("Platform: 142 waiting of 150 at 07:50 · 23 gave up this hour") whenever it sees something there, view on or off. The glows (`src/render/strain.ts`) are fetched the first time the view is turned on and run their own frames only while it shows: soft rectangles on the crowded faces, the stretch of line behind each late departure on the track the train ran, a soft trail with figures walking out into the station's streets (more as more give up), and the outline of a full car park, from amber (`--map-strain-1`) to coral (`--map-strain-2`) by how bad it is. They draw over what's in front, breathe slowly (still with reduced motion), and keep at least 40 px across on screen; a strong one near the camera carries its figure ("142 / 150") on the glass pill, giving way to station names and nearer figures. While Strain shows, the reach glow steps back to a fifth (added to the ground's shader without changing `src/render/reach.ts`). A tap on a glow opens its place's card at half height: a station's, or the service's for a late stretch. Nothing's marked as the fix. Slow sections and held junctions (the line between stations): a section of the player's where trains lose a minute or more this clock hour against its line speed glows along its ballast, and a junction where trains stand held half a minute or more glows along the line through it, each with its card's line ("Trains lost 4 min here this hour", "Trains held 2 min here this hour"). The minutes lost are counted in the view (`StrainRead.slowing`, a few times a second): a train running well clear of the stop it left and the one it's running to (clear of accelerating and braking) slower than nine tenths of its line speed loses the shortfall; the minutes held are the hour's share of the sim's `held`. The `strain` check group covers the view; the `sections` group reads slow sections and held junctions. Full buses and traffic (steps 8, 9 and 12) join it later.

**Where the trouble comes from** (`src/ui/cause.ts`, view only, roadmap step 25). While Strain sees something wrong at a station, its card names the places behind it as chips under the what's-wrong line, at most three, each opening that place's card: "Trains leave full · <service>" for a service whose train calling there or running to it will still have 90% of its seats taken once those getting off there have; "Trains late · <service>" for one that left it late this clock hour; and "Trains slowed · <section>" for the section of line behind the latest of those where trains lost the most time. Facts about the network from Strain's own figures, never a buy to make.

## Terrain

`src/render/terrain.ts`, view only, from the Environment Agency's LIDAR Composite DTM (1 m, Open Government Licence; credited in the footer) by `tools/lidar-to-terrain.mjs`, which fetches it over the WCS into the git-ignored `data/raw/dtm/`. **The railway is the datum**: the tracks, stations and trains stay at y = 0, and the ground is drawn at its real height less the rail's real level (the DTM under the lowest track, bridges and spikes taken out, straight across Stockport Viaduct and the arches from Slade Lane's embankment into Piccadilly, smoothed; Piccadilly's end level, 48 m, is from memory as the DTM there is the undercroft's floor). Away from the line the datum blends smoothly (a point inside a bend is near both arms).

**Tiles**, in game metres, heights in decimetres above Ordnance Datum, in a text encoding (each sample's difference from a plane through its neighbours, one character when small): `src/data/terrain/base.json` comes with the page (its own script file, `terrain-<hash>.js`, preloaded beside the game's): every 128 m over the map (64 m until the Buxton line widened the map and the script before the first frame passed its budget, the `perf` group's 450 KB gzipped), and every 32 m in the kilometre blocks within 1.5 km of the line (the far ring beyond is where only the 48 m base mesh is drawn, seen from a kilometre off), with the rail level every 5 m and the datum every 64 m; 315 KB, about 140 KB gzipped; `src/data/terrain/fine/<i>_<j>.txt`, every 2 m within 100 m of the line, are fetched as the camera comes within about 1.3 km. 1.5 MB in all with the Buxton line to New Mills Newtown, 2.37 MB with the Marple line and the Rose Hill branch (base 354 KB, fine 2,076 KB in 331 tiles); the terrain check holds them to 2.6 MB. The script before the first frame holds only the opening's ground (`docs/decisions/ADR-2026-10-14-budgets-and-the-real-railway.md`): `base.json` has the 32 m blocks within 1.5 km of the line from Edgeley Junction to Piccadilly only, and every line out's 32 m ground, the Buxton line's (1.5 km) among them, the rest's out to 800 m, is in `src/data/terrain/out.json` (134 KB, in 256 m blocks), fetched just after the first frame. Once it's in, with the lines out's kits (Station kits, below), the ground it touches and the stations' pads are laid again a few chunks a look (`TerrainLayer.loadOut`, `layOut`, `outReady`), the roads and stand-in houses stand again on it, the lines out's bridges and arches go up (`buildLandmarks(..., 'out')`), and the bus roads are laid again; the real buildings wait for it. 3.20 MB in all with the Crewe line and its Macclesfield leg (base 292 KB, out 134 KB, fine 2,854 KB in 450 tiles). Each line out is laid like the spine: its own land and strip (`Terrain.lines`; a point belongs to the nearest line, and railway land within 60 m of another line is that line's too, so neither strip draws ground over the other's tracks), its rail level from the DTM under its tracks, straight across its own arches and easing over its last 400 m to the level of the line it joins at its junction, and the datum blended from every line's. Phase 8 adds tiles and levels the same way. `node tools/lidar-to-terrain.mjs --coarsen` gives a base written before the far ring its far ring, without fetching.

**The railway's land** is flat at 0: every track with 5 m of cess, and each station's pad, the ground its kit's parts stand on (`partBoxes`), the edges easing by no more than they run along. The kits are drawn at real scale, so the ground beside the land is read where it is and the real cuttings and banks start at its edge. At a station's edge a step of a metre or more is a brick retaining wall up or down to the ground (Heaton Chapel's cutting: 7.5 m by the bridge, 4 to 5 m at the north end, rising behind the kit's low walls; the pads at Stockport, Levenshulme and Piccadilly stand on walls over the ground below); on the open line the ground is no steeper than a 1.2 cutting slope or a 1:1.5 embankment. Under the viaduct and the arches into Piccadilly there is no land: the ground beneath is the real ground.

The Marple line is wired only from just east of Ashburys in (OpenStreetMap: `electrified=contact_line` with the Glossop line, `no` beyond), so its masts and wires stop there (the corridor's `wires`, metres back from its Manchester end), and the Rose Hill branch has none; along each line out stand trees, signals (no line-speed boards: its real speeds are 30 to 60 mph), a stand-in road on one side and suburban houses, thicker round its stations, until the real surroundings come. Beyond Hazel Grove the Buxton line runs through fields, woods and villages, unwired: no masts or wires there (`src/render/world.ts`, from Hazel Grove's south end), no stand-in roads along it, and only a scatter of houses, thicker round each station by how many live there, until the real surroundings come.

**Drawn** as a base mesh every 48 m over the whole map (in chunks, sunk under the strip near the line, easing to the far ground's level at the map's edge; it takes shadows but casts none) and a strip along the line in 240 m chunks out to 150 m past the land's edge (rows every 16 m coarse, every 4 m fine once the camera is near and the fine tiles are in; its outer edge hangs down to hide the seam). Colours are the `--map-grass`, `--map-grass-2` (two greens drifting into each other), `--map-bank` (steep slopes), `--map-yard` (the tracks' land), `--map-brick` (walls) and `--map-city` tokens; the reach's glow is one shader patch on the shared material. Everything that stands on the ground reads `groundAt` (homes on their lowest corner, on ground level within 4 m; the suburbs' houses the same; the roads along the line and the buses' roads; the camera, never lower than 4 m over the ground beneath it).

## The surroundings

`src/render/buildings.ts`, view only, from OpenStreetMap (buildings, © OpenStreetMap contributors, ODbL) and the Environment Agency's LIDAR Composite first-return DSM less the DTM (1 m, 2022, Open Government Licence) by `tools/osm-to-world.mjs`, which takes an area (stations and a radius, a stretch of corridor, or named squares) and fetches into the git-ignored `data/raw/` (OSM's main API in quarter-square boxes a second apart; the DSM and DTM over the WCS in 500 m chunks). Built in four parts (`docs/briefs/real-surroundings.md`): today's line is in, the 52 kilometre squares within 900 m of the line from Hazel Grove to Piccadilly and 1.4 km of Stockport: 60,600 buildings drawn as 44,100 pieces, 112,800 trees and clumps, and the ground's cover. The lines out are in too (`docs/briefs/real-surroundings-lines-out.md`): 63 more squares within 900 m of the Buxton line from Hazel Grove to New Mills Newtown, the Marple line from Ashburys and the Rose Hill branch (`--corridor 900 --from <id> --to <id>` walks the line the stations are on), with their hedges.

- **Tiles**: `src/data/world/<i>_<j>.txt`, one per kilometre square of the game's frame, a JSON header (where it's from, and what's filled in: how many heights are from OSM, from the LIDAR or guessed, how many roofs assumed) and a line a piece in fixed-width base-90 digits (`decodeWorld`). Fetched after the first frame, nearest the camera first, each waiting for the fine terrain under it. The `world` check group holds them to 2 MB for today's line and 1.5 MB for the lines out (every square not today's); they are 1,462 KB for the lines out, and 1.95 MB (buildings 0.9, trees 0.7, cover 0.35).
- **Where OSM has none**: OSM maps few buildings round Davenport, Woodsmoor and parts of Hazel Grove and Stockport (Davenport's square had 48 for a suburb of semis), so the tool outlines them from the LIDAR too: where the first-return DSM stands 2.5 m or more over the DTM and the laser stopped there (the Agency's last-return DSM within half a metre of the first, as on a roof, where it goes on through a crown), away from OSM's buildings and bridges, each patch fitted as a rectangle if it nearly is one (flag 8 in the tile). 18,550 of today's line's buildings are outlined so; elsewhere they fill the odd unmapped block.
- **Each building**: fitted as an oriented rectangle where it nearly is one (its footprint fills 80% of the smallest rectangle round it; most British houses), its outline otherwise, flat-roofed. Walls from `height`, else `building:levels` at 3 m a storey, else the DSM less the DTM over the footprint less the roof's rise, else 2 storeys (flagged). Roof from `roof:shape`, else pitched for houses (flat over 4 storeys or 30 m deep), else flat where the DSM's top is level, hipped for the deeper ones, pitched for the rest; a pitched roof rises 0.35 of its depth (at most 5 m) along its long side. Houses sharing a wall (two nodes) with like heights and roofs join into rows while the row stays nearly a rectangle (86% full): in the pilot 6,916 of 10,255 buildings are in rows, drawn as 5,371 pieces.
- **Colours**: the suburbs' palette (the photo library's "For the map" lists): red and dark brick most, some render and buff, slate on the gabled roofs and tile on most hips, felt on the flat ones, pale metal on industry's sheds, brick, buff and render on retail's, stone on the churches; within 1.6 km of Piccadilly the city's: Victorian warehouses in red and dark brick, buff and stone, the newer blocks concrete and glass, the tall ones mostly glass. OSM's `building:material` and `building:colour` where it has them. The `--map-bld-*` tokens, each piece a touch lighter or darker.
- **Placed**: moved out from the line as the ground beside it is read (`Terrain.drawnAt`, the terrain's lateral shift inverted), on the lowest ground along its walls (read at the corners and every 8 m between, so a big shed over a dip reaches the ground), its walls sunk half a metre. Left out: anything on the railway's land, within 4 m of it or on its cutting and embankment slopes (`Terrain.railLand`), on a station kit's parts (any, gated or not: the kit draws its own), across the replacement buses' roads, on the Mersey or the M60, or on a landmark's footprint (`standing` in `src/render/landmarks.ts`: the Plaza, St Mary's, the Market Hall, Merseyway, the depots, the Pyramid, the skyline's towers). The stand-in houses, and the town's made-up streets in the viaduct's valley, keep out of every square with a tile, so on today's line they're gone; the landmarks stand as they were. The lines out's stand-ins keep out of their squares the same way, and stay only beyond them.
- **Drawn** merged, one draw call a mesh: within 900 m of the camera in full (pitched, hipped and flat roofs, gable ends), in 250 m chunks built nearest first, a few milliseconds a look, and freed when the camera leaves, casting shadows within 400 m; further off one mesh a square of blocks with flat tops halfway up their roofs. Flat-shaded vertex colours, one material. A square is placed in stages a frame apart (buildings, trees, meshes, cover), about 100 ms here. Low (Fewer people) leaves out the squares past 1.6 km.
- **Trees**, from the same LIDAR: standing 3 m or more where the laser went on through (the last return lower than the first) or the top is rough, clear of every building; one tree at each crown's top (the highest point within a radius growing with height), its spread how far the crown reaches; crowns that overlap join into one clump (a wood, a garden row), up to 16 m across; 4 m or more tall. Instanced: within 550 m one by one, a low-poly crown (20 triangles) on a trunk, casting shadows within 400 m; further off only the clumps 8 m or more across, plainer (8 triangles), which keeps the perf group's triangle budgets. Kept off the railway's land and its slopes, the kits, the landmarks, the river, the M60, the buses' roads and the buildings. Two kinds of crown take the seasons as the lineside trees do (src/render/weather.ts calls `season`): a third blossom in spring and turn rust in autumn, the rest green to gold, bare in winter, snow on them when it lies; each tree a touch lighter or darker. The lineside trees keep out of the squares with tiles. Hedges (`hedges()` in the tool, an `H` line a hedge): OSM's own (`barrier=hedge`), and the edges of OSM's fields, meadows and orchards wherever the LIDAR shows something growing 0.8 to 8 m tall within 1.5 m of the edge and no building, in runs of 12 m or more, each at the median height along it held to 1.2 to 3 m (a hedgerow's taller trees are the trees'). Today's line has almost none; the lines out have 228 km. Drawn as low boxes 1.3 m wide, 10 m at most each on their ground, merged a chunk, only within 900 m of the camera (in full with the buildings, casting shadows within 400 m), in the first kind of crown's colour so they take the seasons; kept off what a tree is kept off, and the buildings.

Painted per square into a 256-pixel layer (4 m a pixel) of one texture array as the square comes in, each point moved out from the line as the ground is, the railway's land cleared; the terrain's material mixes it in (`coverPatch`, after the reach's patch). The `--map-cover-*` tokens. Not a photograph: a few flat colours, as a model railway's ground is painted.

- **The fog**: a square whose every part is deeper from the camera than the fog's far edge (`Fog.far`, at least 1,600 m, nearer in fog and rain) would be drawn in the fog's colour alone, so its far meshes are left out. That keeps the perf group's opening level with the lines out in (Reddish North and Brinnington stand 2 to 3 km from it), and takes 14,000 triangles off the busy save.
- **The tiles' names** are their hash alone (`vite.config.ts`): their list is in the script before the first frame, so each name is kept short.

## Moving around the map

`OrbitRig` in `src/render/camera.ts`, view only: an orbit (where it looks, how far, from which angles; `th` 0 has north, towards Manchester, up the screen) moved like a map app's. On touch one finger slides the map, the ground grabbed staying under it; two fingers pinch towards themselves, twist to turn and, dragged up or down together, tilt (the gesture locks to a tilt or a pinch once the fingers have moved 14 px); a double tap zooms in where it lands. With a mouse, drag slides, right-drag or Ctrl-drag orbits, and the wheel zooms towards the cursor. Arrow keys and WASD move, Q and E turn, + and - zoom, but not while a text field or a control (a button, the sliders) has focus; a tap on the map takes the focus back. A tap that lands on a train (a ray through its bounds, `src/render/pick.ts`) rides alongside it until the player moves; one on a station (its halt and the parts shown, or within 40 px of its label) selects it, so the camera flies there and its panel opens. The slider above the station strip runs the line from Hazel Grove to Piccadilly: dragging it glides the camera along the line at its angle, and it follows the camera otherwise. **Whole line** (`fitAll`) frames the whole network by its extent: the nearest view, from eight turns and three tilts (the camera's own turn unless another is a seventh nearer), that holds every station and its name on the open map, beside or above the sheet; on a screen too narrow for the names (a phone held upright) it holds every station's point and the names that overlap give way. The camera reaches out to 24 km for it, and in to 12 m from where it looks, close enough to stand on a platform beside a train. Far off, the map leaves out what would be a few pixels: a station's kit beyond 9 km of the camera (its name stands for it), the real buildings and trees beyond 9 km, the rails, sleepers, masts, lineside trees and stand-in houses beyond 7 km (`cullCells`), and the base ground beyond 6 km is drawn from every fourth point; so the whole line draws about 260,000 triangles in 290 calls rather than a million in 960. Every fly-to (a station, a card's ghost, a Built chip, Whole line, the compass) eases out, across and in, and lifts on a long move so the player sees where it's going; with reduced motion it's instant. The compass (bottom right of the map) shows while north isn't up, its needle pointing north, and a tap turns north back up. Never lost: the camera stays 4 m over the ground beneath it (Terrain), and where it looks stays within `max(300 m, a third of its distance)` of the line as drawn, slid back along the nearest point rather than stopped.

## Sound

Made in the browser with the Web Audio API (`src/render/sound.ts`), so there are no recordings to license, and changing nothing in the game: it reads the sim and the drawn trains every tenth of a second. A soft bed of distant town is always there (softer at night); trains near where the camera looks rumble by their speed, brake into a platform with a hiss and a squeal, beep their doors, and wind up their motors pulling out; when a train is within 650 m of the owned station under the camera, the speakers chime three notes and murmur a few words (a voice's shape, not speech); birds trill by day and stop at night; rain hisses and patters (Weather and seasons, above). It follows the camera: everything within about 320 m of where it looks, fading out until at whole-line zoom only the bed is left. Browsers allow sound only after a tap, so it starts on the player's first tap or key; muted, it makes nothing. The volume is gentle at the top of the slider (half of full scale).

With one mode there's no kind filter yet; it comes with the first bus route.
- **Half** (58% of the screen, two thirds under 600 px tall): the open place's card, with **Next to** under it: a chip for each place touching it that's owned or offered; a tap flies there and opens it.
- **Full**, or docked: the same, then every place owned or offered listed by kind (Stations, Bus stops, Train services, Depots).

Only its handle (the station's name, a button: Raise, Lower) and header drag it; it snaps to the nearest height, Esc lowers it a step, and with reduced motion it jumps. From 600 px wide, or in landscape, it docks to the left as a side panel (340, 360 or 400 px; `--sheet-w`) with everything in it. It tells the camera how much of the screen it covers (`OrbitRig.cover`), and the camera draws where it looks in the middle of the rest, so focus and fly-tos frame into the open map.

**One card for every place** (`src/ui/panel.ts`, `docs/design/buying.md` section 7): the header names the place, and under it its kind and one figure ("Halt · 3 mi · 40 waiting", "Train service · 2 units", "Depot · spare ready", "Bus station · 31 by bus", "Network · 3 stations"); then four figures for its kind (the rest of a station's behind More), the stages offered as cards, and the Built chips. A place not yet owned shows what it is and Buy, or what it waits on. Tapping a station (chip or map) opens its card at half height; a train opens its service and rides with it; a unit standing in Stockport's sidings opens the depot; a bus stop's bays on the map, or its Built chip on its station's card, open the stop; the cash pill opens the Network card (stations, boarded, earned, reputation, and once Piccadilly is owned Renew the contract). A bus stop is no longer a stage on its station's card: once the station is owned, it's on the row and the station's Next to with its price, and its own card has Buy. A station's card leads with Waiting, Boarded, Revenue (short once it's five figures: £83.3k, £1.25m, so it fits at 320 px) and Gave up, the rest behind More, then its cards (one column, two on a desktop) and the Built chips. Picking a card in portrait sinks the sheet to its peek with a **Buy bar** (the card's name, what it adds, put it back, and Buy or how much to go), so its ghost stands in open map. Docked, the card stays in view with its own Buy.

**Names on the map**: each station's name floats over its kit; where two overlap on screen, the farther gives way. An offered station's name carries its price, bright, with a meter of how near the cash is; a station further out keeps a dim price within 2.5 km of the camera and its name alone beyond (open question 2 of the buying design). **Edge markers** (`src/ui/edges.ts`): up to three offered stations, nearest where the camera looks first, when they're off the open map, wait at its edge with their name and price and an arrow towards them; a tap opens one.

## Settings

A settings sheet opens from the settings button top right (`src/ui/settings.ts`): over the whole screen from the bottom on a phone, centred on wider screens, in two columns on a landscape phone. It holds sound on or off and its volume, the weather (Rain and shine, or Fair weather: Weather and seasons, above), and graphics for older phones: High, Low, or one by one shadows, fewer people (a third of the platform figures, none throwing a shadow) and sharp (drawing at the screen's full pixel ratio; off draws one pixel per CSS pixel, the biggest saving on a phone). Low turns shadows and sharp off and fewer people on. The settings are kept on the device under their own `localStorage` key, `the-commute-prefs-v1` (`src/app/prefs.ts`), never in the save, so starting over keeps them. Under Screen is full screen (`src/ui/fullscreen.ts`, hidden where the browser has none); under Your game, Save to file, Load from file and Start over (`src/ui/saves.ts`), with the line saying the game is ready offline; under About, how it plays and the credits. The far detail the brief also named (the towns and stations further out) is drawn by files other sessions own, so it isn't a setting yet.

## Performance

Playable on a phone from 320 px is the aim, so the `perf` check group (`tools/checks/perf.mjs`) measures three views at 390 × 844 under four times CPU throttling: the opening, the busy save (`src/app/busy.ts`: five stations owned with everything bought, the line built up, the morning peak, rain) at Stockport, and the busy save at Whole line. It logs the draw calls, triangles, geometries, textures and shader programs a frame (`renderer.info` through `window.__view.perf`), the meshes and shadow casters, when the first frame was drawn and the script time a frame spends in the sim, the view's sync and the draw (summed in the loop without allocating), and the gzipped script loaded before the first frame. The calls and triangles are what that frame drew at that view, after the camera's frustum (and the sun's, for the shadows) has culled the rest, with every square of buildings and trees loaded. It fails only on the budgets at its top; frame times are logged, not judged, since the container draws in software at about a frame a second. The triangle budgets are to be set from a real phone (`docs/decisions/ADR-2026-10-14-budgets-and-the-real-railway.md`): until its figures come in they stay as they were. Raise a budget on purpose, in the commit that needs it.

**What one view loads.** The same group opens a fresh page at the opening and with the Buxton and Marple lines framed, and counts every file fetched after the first frame until nothing more comes, against a budget per view (uncompressed, as served by the check's own server). The committed tiles have an allowance per line instead of one total (`tools/lines.mjs`, read by the `terrain` and `world` groups): today's line keeps its own (1.5 MB of terrain, 2 MB of world squares), the Marple line its own (0.75 and 1 MB), and every other corridor about 0.5 MB of each; a tile belongs to today's line's 52 squares or to the line out whose corridor is nearest, and each line's figure is reported. A new corridor gets the default allowance without a change to the checks.

**The stats view.** Opening the site with `?stats` shows a small overlay of what the page costs on that device: frames a second and the slowest frame in the last 10 seconds, the triangles and draw calls of the last frame, the geometries and textures held and the JS heap where the browser gives it, refreshed twice a second. Copy puts one line of it on the clipboard (or selects it to copy by hand), with the screen, the pixel ratio, the shadows and the browser. `?stats&save=busy` opens the busy save in place of the game on the device without touching it: while it's open the save reads as the busy save and nothing is written over the player's own. `src/app/stats.ts` is in the script before the first frame, as a check of the address; the overlay (`src/ui/stats.ts`) loads after it, only with the flag.

What keeps it down, each drawn the same as before: each station's still parts are folded into one mesh per material in each of its groups (the halt, its scenery, each upgrade's), so a station is a few dozen draw calls rather than hundreds, twice over with shadows (`mergeStill` in `src/render/stations.ts`; what life moves, the boards and the shutters, and anything transparent stay as they are, and the ghost, the fog while locked and the rise act on the folded meshes as on the parts). The houses, the trees, the track's ballast and rails and the masts are split by kilometre square (`src/render/cells.ts`), so the camera and the sun's shadow camera each draw the squares they see rather than the whole map's worth. The real buildings are merged a square to a draw call (The surroundings), in full only near the camera; with today's line in, trees and all, a busy save at Stockport draws about 526,000 triangles once every square is in (the buildings 31,000, the trees 36,000). What glows (the signal and platform lamps) and the homes' window bands, which lie on their walls, throw no shadow (`noShadow` in an object's `userData`): about 37,000 triangles of the busy save's shadow pass. The loop makes no arrays or strings a frame where it can help it (a label's text changes only with ownership or the price). The script loads in three files (Terrain, Playing offline). Low turns the shadows off, which halves the draw calls.

## The night

After dusk every bought platform's lamps throw a warm pool of light on it (`src/render/passengers.ts`, the kit's `lamps` parts whose upgrades are bought), fading in as the scene's lamps come on. The platforms empty as the sim's night demand falls; trains still run all night (there's no last train in the sim yet), and the night passes in about two real minutes at 1× (Numbers that matter, Time), so the lamps, the engineering train and dawn show quickly. On a little over half the nights, from 00:45 to 04:45 by the clock, the fast lines close for engineering (`engineering` in `src/render/trains.ts`, seeded by the game's seed and the night): no express runs (they don't come on from 23:00 to 05:30), and a yellow on-track machine (`--map-eng`) with turning orange beacons (`--map-eng-beacon`) crawls a few hundred metres along the fast lines at the night's site (2% of their length over its hours: the night's clock runs so fast that the length of the line went by in a real minute), towards Manchester one night and back the next. It's the view's own and touches nothing in the game.

## Playing offline

Once a player has visited, the game loads and plays without a connection, from the home screen too (the manifest is `public/manifest.webmanifest`, its start URL and icons relative to it, so they work under `/the-commute/` and `/`). `src/app/offline.ts` registers the service worker, `public/sw.js`, after the page has loaded, in a build only (never in `npm run dev`). The build (`vite.config.ts`) fills in the worker's three `build:` lines: the files to cache at install (the page, its scripts, `three-<hash>.js` and `terrain-<hash>.js`, which carries `base.json`, among them, its styles, the icons and the manifest), the fine tiles' names (never inlined into the script, however small: `assetsInlineLimit`), and a stamp hashed from them, so a build that changes nothing changes no worker. The cache is named for the stamp, `commute-<stamp>`; the fine tiles go into `commute-tiles` as the terrain fetches them, so places already seen stay sharp offline, and a tile never seen fails as a failed fetch does now (the base tiles stay). The fonts are kept as they're fetched in `commute-fonts`. Everything the build made comes from the cache first.

**Updates.** The browser looks for a new worker on every visit; a new build's worker installs while the game is open and waits. On the next load of the game, if it's the only tab of it open, the old worker hands over and the page loads again at once from the new build; another tab open keeps the old build until they're all closed. Never mid-game. On taking over, the new worker deletes older builds' caches and the tiles the new build no longer has. The settings sheet says "Ready to play offline" once the worker is in; nothing else shows.

**The checks** get the build on disk: the worker is skipped on any page with `window.__seed` set (every check and screenshot), unless it also sets `window.__offline`, which only the `offline` group does. That group serves `dist/` itself, so it can take the connection away by closing its server (Playwright's offline mode doesn't reach the worker's own fetches), and fakes a new build by serving the worker with another stamp. Saves aren't part of any of this: they stay in `localStorage`.

## Save

`localStorage` key `the-commute-save-v1` (`src/app/storage.ts`): the whole sim state, `SAVE_VERSION` (18: `places`, what's owned and built by place id, in place of `owned`, `ups` and `line`; 12: each train's platform, `pf`, `pfAt` and `bound`, the expresses as trains (`away` while off the map), and `through` at each station and `stats.through` and `stats.express`; 11: `weather`, the spell, its own random numbers and the wet time so far; 10: each station's `homes`, homes that grow; 9: `trouble`, what's going wrong and the replacement buses, and the line's `spare`; 8: each station's `cab` tally and its rank's `cabs`, and `stats.cab`; 7 gave trains their timetable, `due`, `late`, `stood` and `quiet`, and `ends` each service's last departure from each end), the seed and the random state, plus a timestamp. On load the sim advances by the time away at 1× (three sim seconds a real second), capped at half a game day (`MAX_AWAY` in `src/app/clock.ts`). A save that can't be read is kept under a `-broken` key.

**Coming back** (`src/render/away.ts`, view only). After time away the map plays what happened in about three seconds before the toast: the camera starts at Whole line at once (where the network view shows the crowds), a column of coins rises over each station by what it earned while away (one coin from £500, one more for each doubling, at most six; the three biggest earners carry the sum), then at 1.5 s the camera settles back on the station the player left while the homes that grew there rise and its car park fills from where it stood (the homes and car parks are held as they were until then: `HomesLayer.hold`, `StationLife.hold`), and at 3 s the toast says what was earned and the coins fade. If the player moves the camera first, it stays theirs. With reduced motion the coins stand still from the start, the camera cuts back at 1.5 s rather than flying, and the toast comes at 2.2 s. The cap on time away stays half a game day.

**Saves you can carry** (`src/ui/saves.ts`, `toFile`/`fromFile`/`put` in `src/app/storage.ts`). Beside Start over, Save to file downloads the game as `the-commute-day-<n>.json`: the same `{g, selected, at}` the device keeps, written then, a few kB. Load from file opens the picker (no type filter, since some phones grey out JSON files under one) and checks the file without writing anything: not a save, from a newer version, or from an older one with no migration says so in a toast and nothing changes. A good one turns the button into "Tap again to load day 3" for ten seconds; the second tap puts it on the device in place of the game in progress and reloads the page, so it loads as any save does, caught up from when it was written, and nothing writes the old game back over it on the way out. Nothing leaves the device. To extend the format, add a field beside `g` in the file and in `Saved`; `fromFile` carries whatever sits beside `g`.

**The career** (contract renewal) is kept under its own key, `the-commute-career-v1` (`loadCareer`, `saveCareer`, `wipeCareer`), with its own version (`CAREER_VERSION` in `src/sim/contract.ts`); a career from another version starts afresh. `SAVE_VERSION` 14 added the run's `perks` and `stats.late`. Save to file writes `career` beside `g`; loading a file puts its career on the device in place of the one there (one from another version is left behind and the run still loads). Versioning: `docs/decisions/ADR-2026-10-08-no-save-compatibility-before-release.md`.

## Randomness

`src/sim/random.ts`: a mulberry32 whose state is saved, so the same seed and the same purchases give the same game. The bot and the checks rely on it; `tools/rules.mjs` keeps `Math.random()` out of the sim.
