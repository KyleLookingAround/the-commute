# Roadmap

Where this is going, phase by phase, in the order it makes sense to build it. Each phase leaves the game playable and ends with something a player would notice. Phases can overlap where nothing depends on the one before; each says what it needs.

The game is all public transport, not stations alone (the owner, 13 October 2026, `docs/decisions/ADR-2026-10-13-all-public-transport.md`): the railway and the line between stations, buses, trams and the ways to reach them, with the car as the rival. The shape: one line made deep (phase 2); given pressure and a reason to keep playing (3); made complete and alive (4); the lines out of Stockport (5) and the city they all run to (6); trams arriving from the city (7); all of Greater Manchester (8); a passenger's view of it all (9); the stations it could have one day (10); and a very long sci-fi game of eras (11). Every station in every phase starts as a halt and grows, and everything bought shows on the map. Each step below is about one session's work, in the order to build it; S, M and L say roughly how big (a few dollars, about ten, twenty or more). From the second release on, each phase ends with a release.

## 1. Repo and real geography (done)

The line from Hazel Grove to Piccadilly on real geography, playable, with the runbook around it.

- The Hazel Grove branch: the corridor runs on south from Stockport through Davenport and Woodsmoor to Hazel Grove (from the OpenStreetMap export), on two tracks; the three stations come after Piccadilly in the buying order, each with a kit from the export. A second service, the Hazel Grove line, runs from Stockport as far as the branch is owned, turning round in platform 1; passengers between the branch and the main line change at Stockport, walking off one train and through the subway to the other's platform (the panel counts them as Changed). Services are data (`route`, `home`), trains carry their service, and a train running in to the end of its run crosses over to its departure track.
- Boarding through the stop: a train boards while it stands, two doors a car at 0.7 passengers a second each, so the figures file on, a busy stop can leave people for the next train, and longer trains board faster. People reaching the platform while it stands catch it, so peaks lose fewer (lost re-proposed in `tools/baseline.json`; whether peaks should bite harder is the owner's call).
- Real track spacing: the tracks run where the OpenStreetMap export puts them, 1.25 times real across the line at first (about 14 m over four tracks, against 32 m before), and at real scale since 13 October 2026 (the trains, buses, cars and taxis at their real width too, so the stations line up with the real buildings and ground), easing between an open-line layout and each station's own (`layout` in `src/data/network.json`). Stockport is rebuilt round its two wide islands with the 3a bay, platform 0, the carriage sidings and both signal boxes; Piccadilly's twelve bays are six islands and single platforms under four shed spans, trains stopping short of the buffers. The stopper turns round in one platform at each end, so trains no longer jump across on reversing. A new `rails` part draws the tracks nobody runs on. The far ground no longer covers the line from some angles.
- Passengers that move (`src/render/passengers.ts`): figures come in through the station's entrance, walk to a spot on their platform, board the nearest door of a train standing at their face, step off at their destination and walk out, and walk out when they give up; coats in four colours; all cosmetic, in game time. Figures were 1.4 times life size and trains 1.3 times their real height and width; since 13 October 2026 both are life size.
- A photo pass on the kits (geograph.org.uk, via the Commons category lists): cutting walls and the Victorian building at Heaton Chapel, Levenshulme's parapets and platform building, Ardwick's hut and yard shed, Stockport's retaining wall and depot, canopies on Piccadilly's far bays; towers keep clear of stations and stay low-rise around Ardwick.
- The real corridor and stations from OpenStreetMap: `tools/osm-to-network.mjs` gives the shortest rail path from Davenport to Piccadilly (11.5 km, resampled every 60 m) and the station positions; `tools/osm-to-kit.mjs` drafts each kit's platforms, buildings (`footprint` parts from their real outlines), footbridges and subways (`subway` parts, with a stair head on each platform) in the kit's frame. The bot's measures stayed within the baseline on the real geometry.
- The time-of-day control: a slider on the stage locks the light to an hour for a look around and a Live button lets it follow the clock again; the sim's clock never changes (the `daylight` check proves it). Screenshots preset it through `window.__lockHour`.
- A day that shows: the sun rises in the east, crosses the south and sets in the west, orange at the ends and white overhead; one shadow map follows the camera at station zoom (under 900 m out); lamps, windows and lit signs are glassy by day and come on at dusk; stars after dark.
- A sky (`src/render/sky.ts`): a dome with a zenith-to-horizon gradient that follows the clock, a sun disc where the sun light is, soft clouds drifting over the line, and a far ground plane so the land meets the horizon. The stock views look a little lower now so some sky is always in frame; a screenshot can start the camera anywhere through `window.__orbit`.
- Colour: the `--map-*` tokens brightened to a model railway in daylight (greener grass, warmer brick, pale platforms, readable rails) and the sun and sky lights raised to physical units, white overhead and orange near the horizon.
- The runbook from the owner's other games: playbooks, source rules, a seeded sim and a bot, versioned saves, checks and workflows, decision records (`docs/decisions/ADR-2026-10-08-runbook-from-overgrow.md`). TypeScript strict from the first session with a compiler: `src/` and `test/` are strict TypeScript, the build type-checks, and the tools load the sim through `tsx`.
- Vite + Three.js, sim as a tested module, GitHub Pages deploy.
- Track network as corridors + offset tracks + stations at a distance along a corridor, from lat/lon. Branches later become more corridors joined at junctions.
- `tools/osm-to-network.mjs` to get the real corridor shape from an OSM export.
- Station kits as data, with upgrade-gated parts.

## 2. A deep loop on one line (now)

The loop on Hazel Grove to Piccadilly is worth playing for hours before the map grows. Ends with the first shareable release.

- **Stations grow from a halt** (built 11 October 2026, the owner's decision). Every bought station, and Stockport at the start, is a halt: one platform on one line, trains one way. It grows through stages with prerequisites, each a visible stage of its kit: a way across, the second platform (Stockport's platform 3 lets the line run extra units), the booking office, and the rest of Stockport (platforms 0 to 2, which the Hazel Grove line needs) and of Piccadilly (the shed and bays). Reachable stages show with what they open next; the rest stay hidden. Roads, walls, sidings and signal boxes are scenery. The opening re-tuned: more, cheaper steps at Stockport first, and peaks that bite again.
- **Upgrades, better** (built 11 October 2026). A card for each step a station can take next: price, what it adds, what it opens after, and how much to go until the money's there (after overgrow's shed). Tapping a card draws its parts on the map as an amber ghost with the camera on them; buying it makes them rise into place, and a bought station rises out of the fog. Then (11 October 2026): a Built chip frames what it built, the line's upgrades are cards too and frame a train, and the ghost has its own blueprint cyan. Then (12 October 2026): the faster timetable shows on the map as green colour-light signals and 90 boards along the line, ghosted on its card and rising when bought, placed by the view from the corridor with no sim change. A framed train is framed where it was, not followed.
- **Managers and recommendations: not for now** (the owner, 11 October 2026). The player buys every upgrade themselves; no station managers, line controller or highlighted best next buy. `docs/briefs/managers.md` is kept on hold in case that changes.
- **Pressure that bites** (door-to-door demand built 12 October 2026, c479207; measured 11 October 2026). With stations starting as halts the bot loses about 205 of 4,734 over 8 sim hours, all at Stockport's full platform in the first day and a half; no other station passes half full and trains run mostly empty. The owner's answer (issue #2): tune them, so the peaks press on the bought stations too (a halt platform that holds fewer until its second platform is built, a stronger morning peak at the halts, or whatever the bot shows works), with the bot's baselines re-proposed. Built from journey times (12 October 2026, `docs/DESIGN.md`, Journeys): people travel when the whole trip, door to door, fits about 30 minutes each way (Marchetti's constant), so walking, waiting, riding and changing all count, upgrades save minutes, and each group gives up when its wait has used what's left of its budget; lost now spreads to Heaton Chapel and Levenshulme when full trains leave Stockport. The faster timetable brings only about 2% more trips on a line this short: its main gain is more trains an hour from the same units. Later steps: a glow on the map showing each station's 30-minute reach, census population and jobs, and homes that grow where the line brings places within reach.
- **Impacts on the map, not text.** People turning back with a shrug, trains whose windows and riders show how full they are, and a gold coin over the top-earning station (built 11 October 2026; `docs/DESIGN.md`, Pressure on the map). The new stage rising when it's bought belongs to the upgrades work.
- **Credits** (done 11 October 2026). The page footer credits "© OpenStreetMap contributors" (ODbL), linked to their copyright page, and the geograph.org.uk photographs the kits were checked against.
- **Check what was placed at 1.25 times across** (the owner, 13 October 2026). Real scale (509696b) divided the kits and track offsets by 1.25, but some things were placed by eye or by hand against the wider railway and may now stand off their real spot or leave gaps: the landmarks (the bridges over the line and their spans, the viaduct's deck edges, the Ardwick and Marple arches, the town's buildings by the line), the replacement buses' roads where they come down to each station's stop and car park, the lineside masts, signals and boards, the carriage sidings, and anything in a kit's note marked "placed by eye" (Stockport Interchange was already at its real distance). Hold each against OpenStreetMap and `docs/reference/aerial/`, and move what's out. Where it touches the line, done (14 October 2026): the masts, signals and boards, the bridges over the line and the viaduct's deck are all laid from the tracks' own offsets, so they moved with them; the carriage sidings' units stood between the kit's rails (the spare at v −33, the perk's unit at −38.3, the overnight roads out to −65 where the rails end at −52) and now stand on them. Left for its own item: the Ardwick and Marple arches, the town's buildings by the line, and the buses' roads into the stations.
- **The first shareable release.** When the owner calls it: the halt-to-station loop, the upgrades UI, phones checked down to 320 px, What's new, and saves migrated from then on. The full loop (issues, specs, PRs, look backs) switches on (the `release` playbook).

## 3. Pressure and the long loop

The line you've built gets trouble worth fixing as it gets busy, a reach you can watch grow, and a contract that ends and starts you again a little stronger. Built on phase 2's door-to-door demand: everything here acts through journey times. Mostly sim work, so it runs beside phase 4's view work. Ends with the second release.

1. **The reach on the map** (M, view, built 12 October 2026, `docs/DESIGN.md`, Journeys). A soft glow round each station over the homes within about 30 minutes of where they're going; it grows when something you buy saves minutes, and a card's preview rings where it would reach. A Minutes control shades the ground by minutes to Manchester. Cards say what they save in minutes and for how many homes. A fresh Stockport reaches about 570 m; the Hazel Grove branch has none until its waits come down. Later: framing a card's preview so its ring shows beside the parts it adds (the camera frames the parts, so the ring shows once you pull back); a near side and a far side to the glow, since the far side walks round until there's a way across (the sim keeps one average walk); homes that grow can read `reachOf` for where the line brings homes within reach.
2. **Late trains** (M, built 12 October 2026, `docs/DESIGN.md`, Timetable). A long stop at a busy platform makes a train late, and the lateness carries down the line; late trains use up passengers' budgets, so peaks press hardest where trains bunch. Shown on the train as an amber lamp over the cab and, once phase 4 has them, the departure boards (`Train.late`, `Train.due`). Units now keep an even gap, which took most of the bot's lost passengers away (about 350 to 80); longer trains ease lateness most, extra units next, and the faster timetable only by its 30 s more recovery at each end. Later: the second platform letting a train stand clear of the line (trains don't block each other at platforms yet), and whether the peaks should press harder again now the units are spaced.
3. **Platforms that block, and peaks that press again** (M, built 12 October 2026, `docs/DESIGN.md`, Platforms). One train at a platform face at a time: a train for a stop whose faces are taken stands at its signal. A face is each way's one platform at a through stop, and where trains turn round, a halt's one platform, a second with the second platform (Stockport's platform 3), more with the rest of the station. On the bot's line it never engages, since units keep seven minutes or more apart and a stop overruns two at most; it holds trains once they bunch. The peaks press again through patience instead: no grace past the budget, and a wait past the timetabled gap felt at three times riding, so lost is about 200, nearly 40% beyond Stockport. Extra units relieve it most, canopies and the faster timetable a little; longer trains only once trains run full; lifts and second platforms bring more people than they keep, so they want the platform extension after. Later: a second face each way (a loop or a bay) at a through stop, and extra units before platform 3 now Stockport's one face would hold them.
4. **Taxi ranks** (S, built 12 October 2026, `docs/DESIGN.md`, Taxi ranks). A stage a station can buy where the real one has a rank or forecourt (Stockport, Heaton Chapel, Levenshulme, Piccadilly, Hazel Grove), £1,200: six cabs queue outside the entrance and take some of the people who'd otherwise give up at a full platform or a late train, for a cut of the fare, until a busy peak empties the rank. On the bot's line about 30 go by cab, a fifth of those who give up, and Stockport's rank pays for itself in about two game days. The rank and the cabs coming and going show it. The station panel shows how many went by cab (By cab) where there's a rank. Later: the cabs' road movement serves the replacement buses of step 5.
5. **Things going wrong** (L, built 12 October 2026, `docs/DESIGN.md`, Things going wrong). A failed train stands with its hazard lights flashing and blocks its line for a few minutes, a signal failure has trains crawl through a stretch at caution; none in the first sim hour, about two in 8 sim hours, by day, one at a time. A spare unit (stabled in Stockport's sidings, offered once a train has failed) halves the rescue; the faster timetable's new signals stop most signal failures. When a failure blocks the line, rail replacement buses with a "Rail Replacement" board shuttle between the stops outside the stations either side along the roads (from memory for now: OpenStreetMap couldn't be reached; `tools/osm-to-roads.mjs` redraws them from an export). On the bot's line one at a peak costs about 80 to 160 people, mostly at Stockport, and the buses carry a few dozen. Later: ~~departure boards showing "cancelled" or "bus replacement"~~ (built, The line, polished); ~~the spare unit visibly driving out to the failed one; buses driving in from and off to a depot instead of appearing at the stop~~ (built, The line, polished); the roads from OpenStreetMap. The line panel's heading counts who went by bus once any have.
6. **Real people** (M, built 12 October 2026, `docs/DESIGN.md`, Homes and draw). Where people live and where they work from Census 2021, turned into each station's homes and draw by `tools/census-to-network.mjs`, in place of the hand-set numbers; credited on the page. Later: buses and trams as competition beyond Stockport's own buses, a normal year's jobs in the city centre once a post-lockdown source is open, and the city's other stations taking their own areas in phase 6.
7. **Homes that grow** (M, built 12 October 2026, `docs/DESIGN.md`, Homes that grow). Where the line brings places within reach, houses and flats appear over the game days, and the homes in the reach grow with them: improve the line and the suburbs visibly fill in, as Marchetti's constant says they do. Each station's homes grow towards a ceiling from the census (half as many again as live within a walk), faster the better its trip; new terraces and flats rise round it, one for every 3 homes. The station panel's Homes figure reads the homes now. Later: what contract renewal (step 9) does to grown homes.
8. **Saves you can carry** (S, built 12 October 2026, `docs/DESIGN.md`, Save): Save to file and Load from file beside Start over, the file the same shape the device keeps, checked before it replaces anything; still never on a server.
9. **Every platform in use** (M, built 12 October 2026, `docs/DESIGN.md`, Platforms and Expresses). Each service has the platforms it really uses (the owner: Stockport's Manchester stoppers use 3 and 4; Wikipedia for the rest, in the network data's `platformNote`) and takes whichever is free, its first choice first, a turning list shared out by unit: with two units the stoppers turn in Stockport's 4 and 3 by turns, a third in the bay 0, and at Piccadilly in 5 and the bays 11, 12 and 4. Avanti West Coast and CrossCountry now call at Stockport (3 north, 2 south, once built; through until then) and Piccadilly, take platforms like any train, and pay an access charge and a share of their long-distance passengers' fares, about £3,500 in the bot's 8 sim hours. The boards give each train's platform and an express's destination. Later: a loop or bay at a halt (a second face one way, so a train can wait while another passes; looked at in Loose ends and handed on, as no halt on the line has the room); ~~passengers walking to the platform their train stands at~~ (built, The line, polished); Piccadilly's other bays for more services; real express frequencies once the timetable is more than one cycle.
10. **Car parks and bus links** (M, built 12 October 2026, `docs/DESIGN.md`, Car parks and bus links). Later: Heaton Chapel's and Davenport's car parks (they have drive pools; a photo to settle where), their bus stops (their surroundings already ride to Stockport), the Interchange's flats, helix ramp and Station Road link, the drivers' and bus riders' way home in the evening, ~~Levenshulme's forecourt and taxi rank at street level~~ (built, The line, polished). The station's surroundings bring people (the owner, 12 October 2026): a car park stage, or a bigger one, widens the reach to drivers from further out (park and ride, felt in door-to-door time); a bus link (Stockport's Interchange first, drawn when it's bought, then stops at the other stations; the town centre's seeded filler blocks, `town()` in `src/render/landmarks.ts`, fill the valley it stands in, so add its footprint to `taken` there) brings people from beyond a walk, as the census tool's bus hubs already do for Stockport; both shown on the map, the car park filling and the buses coming in.
11. **Shorter days, quick nights** (M, built 12 October 2026, `src/sim/day.ts`, `docs/DESIGN.md`). The owner (12 October 2026): the day was long and the night pointless to wait through. The clock keeps its old rate through the peaks (from 06:00), runs twice as fast between them and ten times through the night (23:00 to 05:00), eased so nothing jumps: a game day is 45 real minutes at 1× instead of 80 (15 at 3×), the night about 2, and a » by the clock while it races. Each clock hour carries the trips it did, so a day earns about the same (2.5% less, fewer trains between the peaks). Later: ~~the real last and first trains (stations empty through the night)~~ (built, Loose ends); ~~the expresses still come by sim time, so a quarter fewer call a game day~~ (on the clock, Loose ends).
12. **Contract renewal** (L, built 12 October 2026, `docs/DESIGN.md`, Contract renewal), the first layer of the owner's prestige idea. Once Piccadilly is owned the line panel offers to renew the contract whenever the player likes: the reputation earned (passengers carried, less those who gave up and late departures) is banked and buys perks in four branches on a renewal sheet (starting cash, quicker building, a wider reach, a unit in the sidings and a spare), and the next run starts again from Stockport as halts, the stages folding back down on the map and a plaque by Stockport's entrance counting the contracts. The career is kept apart from the run; `tools/bot.mjs --runs 2` plays whole contracts: a first run banks about 62 and the second reaches Piccadilly about a quarter sooner. Pacing per run is proposed, not yet agreed with the owner. Later: what the milestones unlock (they're recorded, nothing reads them yet); a perk that moves where a run starts or what's fogged (the owner, 13 October 2026: renewals unlock different starting positions later, while the first game always opens the same way, on the gridlock if that design is built); the renewal as a longer scene (trains running back to the depot); eras and challenges on the same career (phase 11).
13. **Loose ends** (M, built 13 October 2026, 96dfd1c, 86212cb, 46e43ef): the expresses by the clock, one of each every two clock hours through the real day (express takings a game day back to about what they were before shorter days); real last and first trains, 05:30 to 23:30, with the units stood overnight in Piccadilly's bays, Stockport's carriage sidings and at Hazel Grove, and the boards carrying the next run to the first train; no loop at a halt, since no halt on the line has the room (Heaton Chapel and Levenshulme are four tracks and two platforms, the rest double track). Later: Hazel Grove's carriage sidings drawn; the quick night passes in less than one run along the line, so units stand only briefly (stations empty for the night would need a clock decision); real express frequencies (Avanti three an hour, CrossCountry two); a stabled unit driving in to its platform at 05:30; whether three units at one end bunch at dawn.
14. **The second release.**

## 4. The line, complete and alive

Every station on the line as it stands today, in full, and a line that feels alive at every hour. Mostly view work in kits, the renderer and the panels; it can run alongside phase 3 now. Ends with a release.

1. **More to see when bought** (M, built 12 October 2026, `docs/DESIGN.md`, Station life): departure boards reading the timetable and late trains, shops lit by day and shuttered at night with a queue when busy, car parks filling with the hour, cycle racks, platform extensions rising with their lamps, and a check that every upgrade shows. Later: car parks at Heaton Chapel, Levenshulme and Davenport once photos say whether they have one; ~~boards showing the calling points~~ (built, The line, polished).
2. **Tracks, trains and stations right** (M, built 12 October 2026, `docs/DESIGN.md`, Where the tracks are): a geometry check that every platform edge sits 2.1 m from its track all along, no train sweeps through anything solid, and every train stands wholly alongside its platform. The line is drawn straight through each station's platforms, each car rides its own bogies, crossovers come before the platform, expresses stand at Piccadilly, and four stations' stops moved to the middle of their platforms. Later: compare Piccadilly's multi-storey and cycle hub, and the kits' other from-memory parts, with OpenStreetMap when a session can reach it.
3. **Stations as they are today** (L, built 12 October 2026, `docs/DESIGN.md`, Station kits): every station held against photographs from geograph and Commons, recorded in each kit's note. Ardwick's rust-brown lattice footbridge with open flights (`lattice`, `flights`); Levenshulme's way in on the east, its barrel-roofed shelters (`vault`) and its car park; Heaton Moor Road on brick arches (`arches`) with the ramp under its west arch (`ramp`); Stockport's round glass entrance on Station Road (`drum`) and navy canopy columns; Davenport and Woodsmoor reached by steps and ramps from their road bridges, not footbridges; Hazel Grove's north footbridge the public one; Piccadilly's platforms 13 and 14 with their walkway and lounge, Gateway House (`floors`), and the multi-storey car park where it stands. Later: the bays' splay (the photos can't settle it); Piccadilly's platform numbers in its note run the other way from the real ones; Davenport's car park (which side); ~~the arched recesses in Levenshulme's embankment~~ (built, The line, polished) and the cuttings' real depth with the terrain; ~~hipped roofs (Davenport, Hazel Grove, Heaton Chapel's booking office) as a roof part~~ (built, The line, polished).
4. **Landmarks** (M, built 12 October 2026, `docs/DESIGN.md`, Landmarks): the viaduct for real (27 arches on full-height piers over the Mersey, the 1880s widening's seam, floodlit), the M60 with its traffic and the river beneath, the Pyramid, the Plaza, Merseyway, St Mary's, the Market Hall and a town centre round them, and the city skyline (Beetham Tower, Deansgate Square and five more) on the horizon, scaled along the line of sight so it sits right from anywhere. Later: the Plaza's and Merseyway's detail from closer photos; the city's other towers in phase 6.
5. **Terrain** (L, built 12 October 2026, `docs/DESIGN.md`, Terrain) from the Environment Agency's open LIDAR: the Mersey valley under the viaduct (now at its real span, with the town, the Pyramid, the river and the M60 at their real positions), the cuttings at Heaton Chapel and Davenport, the Levenshulme embankment, the line's real level as the datum, retaining walls and slopes from the height difference, the camera following the ground. Base tiles with the page, fine tiles by zoom, so phase 8's county map reuses them. Later: ~~the river's and the M60's real courses; the brick arches from Ardwick into Piccadilly~~ (built, The line, polished); ~~Levenshulme's arched recesses, which want its forecourt, car park and taxi rank at street level~~ (built, The line, polished); ~~the replacement buses' roads drop down a station pad's wall where they leave it~~ (built, The line, polished).
6. **Life** (M, built 12 October 2026, `docs/DESIGN.md`, Who's travelling, Sound, Settings and The night): passengers dressed by who's travelling (commuters at the peaks, students with suitcases towards Piccadilly, cyclists with bikes, a seeded match day's crowd in club colours), sound made in the browser that follows the camera (trains, doors, a chime and murmur when a train is due, birds by day, quiet at night), lamp-lit platforms after dark and an engineering train on the fast lines in the small hours, and a settings sheet: sound and volume, and graphics for older phones (shadows, fewer people, sharpness). Later: far detail as a setting once the world's files are free, real last trains so stations stand empty after them (sim work), and match days that change demand.
7. **Weather and seasons** (M, built 12 October 2026, `docs/DESIGN.md`, Weather and seasons): spells of sun, cloud, rain, fog and winter snow from their own seeded random numbers, felt through journey times (rain makes the walk and an open wait feel longer, so canopies and lifts pay more; leaves slow autumn's trains; snow slows everything and keeps a few at home), gently: about 6% of a year's takings. On the map, rain and snow falling round the camera, clouds thickening, fog closing in, wet platforms and settling snow, umbrellas up, trees along the line through the year, and dusk early in winter; rain in the sound; a Fair weather setting for the look. Later: storm season as a challenge (phase 11); puddles' sheen beyond the platforms' tint; the trees as a terrain layer from real woodland data; match days and the weather keeping people at home on the same day; the departure boards saying when leaves slow trains.
8. **Moving around the map** (M, built 12 October 2026, `docs/DESIGN.md`, Moving around the map). Today you can only orbit and zoom round one spot, and get anywhere else through a card or Whole line. Move like a map app: on touch, one finger slides across the map, two fingers pinch to zoom, twist to turn and drag up or down to tilt; on a desktop, drag to slide, right-drag to orbit, the wheel zooms towards the cursor, and the arrow keys and WASD move. Zoom towards the finger, not the middle of the screen. Glide along the line: the station strip doubles as a slider down the line, tapping a station on the map flies there and opens its panel, double-tap zooms in, and tapping a train rides alongside it until you move. Fly-tos ease out, across and in (at once with reduced motion); a compass puts north back; the camera stays above the ground and near the line, so you can't get lost. Whole line fits the whole network by its extent, every station on screen, and leaves out far detail so it stays quick (13 October 2026, the pick-up's item 4).
9. **Full screen** (S, built 12 October 2026, `src/ui/fullscreen.ts`, checked by the `fullscreen` group). A button (in settings, under Screen, since the redesign) puts the whole page in full screen and takes it out again, hidden where the browser can't (iPhone); a web app manifest (`public/manifest.webmanifest`, `display: fullscreen` falling back to `standalone`) and the iOS meta tags let it be added to the home screen and open without the browser's bars; the page keeps clear of the notch and home indicator on all four sides. The icon is a train on the viaduct in the game's colours, drawn in `public/icon.svg` with PNGs rendered from it. Later: the redesign may move the button; offline play is step 11.
10. **UX/UI redesign, phones first** (L; stage 1, the directions page, built 12 October 2026: https://kylelookingaround.github.io/the-commute/design/ and `docs/design/redesign.md`, recommending A, the platform sheet; stage 2, A, built 13 October 2026: `docs/DESIGN.md`, The interface, from `docs/briefs/redesign-build.md`). A fresh design of the whole interface for one-handed play in portrait, landscape and down to 320 px, with tablets and desktop following: the top bar, the station and line panels, the cards, the stage's controls. A new player's first five minutes (what to tap first, and why it matters) without a tutorial wall. Contrast, keyboard and reduced motion. ## 7. Trams

Metrolink arrives as a second network on the same map, and the long-held goal: trams to Stockport. Needs the city's streets (phase 6) and the road network begun for replacement buses (phase 3). Ends with a release.

1. **Trams as their own thing** (L): a second kind of vehicle (shorter, quicker away, running in the street), roads as a network the trams run along, junctions and traffic lights, stops as kits (side or island platforms in the street, shelters, ticket machines) that grow from a single stop like stations do, a depot and a tram fleet.
2. **Where they come from** (L): Piccadilly's own tram stop, under the station in the undercroft. The first tram purchase is that stop, then the line across the city centre (Piccadilly Gardens, Market Street, St Peter's Square, Deansgate-Castlefield), then south through Chorlton and Didsbury to East Didsbury, the end of the real line, about two and a half miles west of Stockport.
3. **The goal** (M): extend from East Didsbury to Stockport, a real proposal, ending at a new tram stop beside the station; Stockport's tram stage when it arrives.
4. **How they play** (M): frequent and cheap to run, feeding the railway at interchanges (Piccadilly, Victoria, Deansgate, East Didsbury's park and ride, then Stockport); the single tram route across the city centre as the pressure, and a second city crossing to relieve it.

## 8. Greater Manchester

The whole county on one map: ten boroughs, every railway and tram line, opened up region by region. Ends with a release.

1. **The map at county scale** (L, first): the world in tiles loaded by zoom (building on phase 4's terrain tiles), national terrain, the M60 all the way round as the county's ring with the motorways out (the M56, M61, M62, M66, M67, the A6(M) and the A627(M)), towns drawn by density from land use instead of house by house, stations as markers far out with their kits up close, trains as moving lights at county zoom; a county-wide OpenStreetMap pipeline with checks; a frame-time and memory budget on phones, checked.

## 9. Passenger mode

A passenger's view of the network you built. Can start any time after phase 4, and grows with the map.

- Walk the stations and ride the trains (and later the trams) in first person as one of your own passengers.
- Journeys with a brief ("Levenshulme to Stockport by 08:45 with a suitcase", later across the county with changes), scored on time and stress; every decision felt from the other side.
- Reports from journeys feed back into the tycoon: spot a problem, get a discount on the fix.

## 10. Stations that don't exist yet

Once a station is complete as it stands today, it keeps growing into versions that have been planned, proposed or are plausible, each still recognisably the real place underneath.

- **Planned or proposed for real:** Piccadilly's platforms 15 and 16, a high-speed station alongside Piccadilly and Northern Powerhouse Rail across the north, Stockport's new interchange with more through platforms, reopened stations such as Reddish South, more Metrolink (the Airport's western leg, Middleton, Heywood), tram-trains running onto the Hazel Grove, Marple and Glossop lines, a tunnel under the city centre.
- **Plausible:** buildings over the tracks, solar canopies and roof gardens, a glass roof over Heaton Chapel's cutting, double-deck platforms, automatic trains every two minutes, the viaduct widened again.
- **Trains and towns move on with them:** newer units, the city end growing taller, more people living along the lines.

## 11. The very long game: sci-fi eras

Past the plausible the network goes science fiction, still a model railway in daylight rather than anything bleak, and the deeper layers of the owner's prestige idea (11 October 2026) become the heart of the long game, on top of phase 3's contract renewal.

- **Eras:** the trains (electric units, then maglev on the viaduct, then pods in tubes, then lines in the sky), the stations (towers, then sky ports), the sky (airships by day, lit traffic lanes at night); the commute shrinking from Stockport to Piccadilly in minutes to one.
- **Eras as the longer loop:** start again in the next technology with heritage earned from the last; each era has its own stations and vehicles, and the old ones stay on the map as a heritage line and museum by the viaduct.
- **Challenges:** runs with a twist (storm season, strike days, no barriers, one unit only), each with a lasting reward.
- **What it needs:** the bot playing whole loops of renewals and eras; pacing targets per layer agreed with the owner.

## 12. Beyond Greater Manchester (the very long term)

The owner, 13 October 2026: the game could one day cover the whole of the UK, but Greater Manchester comes first and comes right, with every detail checked, before the map grows past it. Nothing here starts until phase 8 is done and its performance holds on phones.

1. **Other cities as starting positions:** a renewal (the prestige layer) unlocks a fresh run in another city, such as Leeds, Liverpool, Birmingham or Glasgow, each a city-sized map built by the same tools from the same open data (OpenStreetMap, the Environment Agency's LIDAR or its equivalents, the census, NaPTAN). Starts are data, not code (`docs/briefs/gridlock-opening.md`).
2. **Station kits generated at scale:** the kit tool drafts every station from OpenStreetMap, and only the big ones are finished by hand, each with its `note`.
3. **Joining the cities up:** intercity services between the cities the player has built, drawn at whole-line detail between them, with a coarser long-distance journey layer in the sim and full detail only where the player is.
