## A correction to the first pass

The first pass said the late game's collapse came with nothing on offer to fix it. **That was wrong.**

- On day 7 the bot still runs **two units with the first longer trains**, holding over £200,000.
- Its policy never buys the third unit (£50,000) or the second longer trains (£40,000). It stops at `units < 2` and `cars < 1` (`tools/bot.mjs`).
- Buying both once Piccadilly is owned and there's £70,000 to spare, on seeds 1–3:

| Seed | Lost, days 6–7, bot as is | With the third unit and longer trains | On time (within 5 min), days 6–7, as is | With them |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | 3,771 | 1,533 | 39% | 99% |
| 2 | 6,353 | 2,403 | 42% | 77% |
| 3 | 6,493 | 2,520 | 21% | 86% |

With them, 13 to 16% more people board. So the game has the fix; the bot doesn't take it.

This means:
- **The baselines measure a player who never buys the obvious fix.** The 16 h figures for lost, lateness and cash (and so the case for Delay Repay) are set by that gap.
- **The fix lives on the stopper's card**, reached by tapping a train or its chip. A player looking at Stockport's card, with 164 waiting and a full car park, sees an upper deck, lifts and Greggs. Nothing on the station's card names where the trouble comes from.

## The ideas, ranked again

Unchanged ideas keep their first-pass write-up. Only what's new or moved is written out here.

1. **Teach the bot every buy** (improves the bot, S; new step 24, built first).
   - **What.** The bot's policy buys:
     - the third unit and the second longer trains when its trains leave full or late
     - lifts and retail where they pay back within a game day or two
   - Then the baselines are re-proposed at 8 and 16 h.
   - **Why.** Every later balance call (steps 18 to 20 and services' frequencies) is judged against the bot. Today it plays a game no sensible player would.
   - **Risk.** It moves the baselines on purpose; the commit says why.
   - **What it changes.** `tools/bot.mjs` and `tools/baseline.json` only. It moves the bot item out of step 23.
2. **Stages that keep going** (step 18, unchanged). With the fix bought the bot still ends 16 h holding £56,000 to £390,000 and buys nothing it needs, so the money glut stands.
3. **Show where the trouble comes from** (improves the card's "what's wrong now", S; new step 25).
   - **What.** When a station's trouble comes from another place, its card names that place as a chip that opens its card. For example:
     - "Trains leave full · Stopper": the stopper's card has the extra unit and longer trains
     - "Car park full": the car park's next stage is on the same card, as now
   - **Why.** Seeing the cause is the step before buying the fix. It's a fact about the network, not a recommended buy, so it keeps the owner's rule. Serves: phones first (one tap from where the player is looking), impacts readable.
   - **What it changes.** The card's strain line in `src/ui/`, reading what Strain already works out.
   - **Risk.** None for perf.
4. **Late trains cost money** (step 19, reframed). The collapse is fixable, so Delay Repay's job is to make lateness felt, so a player goes looking for the fix. It still gives late cash a purpose, and the case holds. It should be built after step 24, so its balance is judged against a bot that buys the fix.
5. **Gridlock that keeps moving** (step 20, unchanged). Drives off are still 15 to 35% at 16 h, and the M60 stays at 1.2.
