# Ideas review, 14 October 2026

The first pass of the ideas session (`docs/briefs/ideas.md`), on `main` at 633f5fa. It ranks what would make the game better, most valuable first, against the owner's direction: depth before breadth, an incremental upgrade game, all public transport with the car as the rival, impacts on the map, no advisor, colourful and alive, phones first.

The work underway is left out: real services and frequencies, the expresses and platform capacity (services), the stats view and check speed (budgets), the gridlock opening G2 to G4, and the fixes round the stations (station surroundings).

## What playing it showed

**How it was played.** The bot ran on seeds 1–3 to 8 and 16 sim hours; every figure below is from it, all within today's baselines. The page was then loaded from the bot's game on seed 1 at 0.3, 2, 6 and 14 sim hours, at 390 px portrait and 1440 px desktop: the home view, Whole line and the Network card.

- **The opening is good.** There's a buy at once and the next soon after. Heaton Chapel comes at 0.6 h and Piccadilly at 5.7 h. The longest stretch of daytime with nothing bought is about 0.8 sim h: 16 real minutes at 1×, 1.6 at 10×.
- **Every station plays the same.** Each one buys the same five stages in the same order within about an hour of opening: way across, second platform, booking office, barriers, canopy. Only names and prices differ. Stage prices are flat (£1,200 to £9,000) everywhere and for the whole game.
- **The late game has more money than things to buy.** At 14 sim hours the bot holds £230,000 while earning about £340,000 a game day. Stockport's card offers an upper deck for £20,000, lifts for £6,000 and Greggs for £9,000. The next stations cost £280,000 to £380,000, so the player waits for a station that comes about every 2.5 sim hours. Cash at the end of 16 h is £148,000 to £175,000.
- **The network breaks late and the player can't see why.** Days 1 to 4 are calm. By day 6 the peaks lose 600 to 1,500 people each:
  - day 7's evening peak loses 450 to 600 at Piccadilly alone
  - by day 7 only 0 to 13% of main-line departures leave within 5 minutes; the worst are 17 to 24 minutes late
  - Piccadilly's platform 5 carries about 195 departures over the run, against about 20 for its next busiest
  - none of the cards on offer fixes this, and the bot, flush with cash, has nothing that does

  This is the late-game pressure the roadmap wants. But it comes as a collapse, not a problem to solve.
- **The car barely moves.** At 16 h, cars off the road are 0.6 to 4.1%, and the drives the network could take, 15 to 35%. The M60 sits at 1.19 to 1.2 from first peak to last. The A6 at Mersey Square ends at 0.93 on two seeds but 1.12 on seed 1. Once the gridlock opening makes this the score on the map, most of the map's roads will hardly change after the first morning.
- **Whole line hides the network.** At Whole line on both phone and desktop:
  - the clouds sit between the camera and the ground, as white blobs over everything
  - the tracks are hairlines on a flat green plane
  - trains, crowds and strain don't show; only the name labels read

  This is the view a player on a phone opens to see how things are going.
- **The Network card is thin.** At £2m earned it shows four figures and Renew. Nothing there shows how the network is doing, or how it has grown.
- **Coming back is a text toast.** "While you were away: £… from … passengers" is the only sign of time away. It's capped at half a game day.
- **The night** takes about two real minutes and is fine as it is.

## Calls taken

The owner let this session add to the roadmap and settle calls without asking (14 October 2026). This pass takes these, each marked in the roadmap:

- **Delay Repay refunds fares** (idea 1). It's gentle, since the early game runs on time; the build must hold the 16 h baselines and re-propose them with the bot's table.
- **Tiers are priced from what a station earns** (idea 2). What's drawn still follows the real place.
- **The away cap stays at half a game day** (idea 4). The map's welcome back comes first; raise the cap only if players ask.
- **G5 is built** (idea 5). The gridlock design left it for the owner to ask for; the bot's flat car figures are the reason to build it.
- **Feeder buses come before timetabling** (idea 7).
- **Contract targets aren't an advisor** (idea 9): they say what the franchise wants, never what to buy.

No owner's preference in the project notes changes.

## The ideas, ranked

Each says whether it **improves** something that exists or is **new**.

### 1. Late trains cost money: Delay Repay and a punctuality score (new, M)

- **What.** The real railway's two lateness costs, made the game's:
  - **Delay Repay.** Northern refunds a passenger whose train arrives 15 minutes or more late. Here each such rider takes back part of their fare.
  - **The contract scored on punctuality.** The share of trains within 5 minutes, the railway's PPM, sets a bonus or a cut on the reputation the contract banks.
- **On the map.** A late train sheds small red coins back towards its platform as it arrives. A station that loses a lot glows red in Strain. The Network card shows punctuality and what the day's Delay Repay cost.
- **Why.** It turns the late game's collapse into a cost the player feels and can fix. It gives the late game's cash a purpose: the fixes for lateness (more units, longer trains, resignalling, flyovers, and the platform capacity the services session adds) start to pay for themselves. It also makes running a line well count, which the owner's idea of contract renewal already wants. Serves: pressure, incremental (a reason to buy), impacts on the map.
- **What it changes.** Fares, through a refund a passenger: about `src/sim/sim.ts`, `contract.ts` and the reputation sum. Reputation already takes 20 for each departure more than 5 minutes late; this replaces that with the punctuality share.
- **Risk.** Pacing: it takes money away late in the game, so it needs the bot at 16 h, and maybe 32 h. Early it's nearly nil, since days 1 to 4 run on time. No phone or perf cost beyond a few instanced coins.
- **Owner's call.** Is losing money to lateness acceptable in an incremental game? The gentler option: no refund, and punctuality only moves reputation.

### 2. Stages that keep going, and one stage each station has alone (improves stations, M to L)

- **What.** Two changes to what a station can buy:
  - **Tiers.** The stations' everyday stages get further levels, priced from what the station earns rather than a flat price. For example:
    - canopy, then full-length canopies
    - booking office, then a staffed travel centre
    - Greggs, then a row of shops
    - platform extension, then 6-car platforms, then 8
    - cycle racks, then a cycle hub

    Each tier shows: longer canopies, more shopfronts, a cycle hub with bikes. At any time, every station has a next thing to buy, sized to the cash a player of that station has.
  - **A signature stage per station,** from its real history or real plans, so stations stop feeling like the same five buys. For example:
    - Heaton Chapel's Victorian building restored, its canopy valance and colours back
    - Levenshulme's market by the station on Saturdays: a surge to the stop, and stalls on the map
    - Stockport's Town Centre West footbridge and the viaduct floodlit: the town's regeneration bringing homes
    - Ardwick's depot open day
    - Piccadilly's Fairfield Street entrance
  - Each needs a `note` from its source.
- **Why.** This is the incremental genre's core: something to buy at every scale. Today stages are a five-item checklist done in six minutes, and the late game waits on £300,000 stations. Serves: incremental, depth before breadth, everything bought shows.
- **What it changes.** `UPGRADES` and `grow` in the network data, the kits' gated parts, the bot's policy and baselines. It may replace the flat retail stage with tiers.
- **Risk.** Pacing (the bot needs a policy for tiers). Perf: more gated parts at the busy save, kept to merged meshes like today's stills.
- **Owner's call.** Should prices scale with a station's takings (the idle-game way) or follow real costs? This review's view: scale with takings, with the size of what's built following the real place.

### 3. A network view you can read on a phone (improves Whole line, M)

- **What.** At wide zoom, the map should read like Mini Metro on top of the 3D.
- **What it shows.**
  - the clouds fade out above a height
  - each service's line drawn bold in its own colour, from the corridors
  - trains as moving dots, coloured by how full they are
  - stations as rings sized by who's waiting, red when people are giving up
  - the Strain glows and the roads' load wash readable at that height
- **Tap.** A station or a train opens its card, as now.
- **Why.** A five-minute phone session starts here: "how's my network?" should take one glance. Today it shows labels over clouds. Serves: phones first, impacts on the map, colourful.
- **What it changes.** Whole line in `src/render/`, the clouds' fade, the camera's far detail. It takes in phase 8 step 5's schematic view early, for today's map.
- **Risk.** Small for perf: lines and dots are a few instanced meshes, and far detail is already left out at that zoom.
- **Owner's call.** None, but it's a look, so the mockup goes to the owner.

### 4. Come back to a changed map (improves the away catch-up, S)

- **What.** On loading after time away, before the toast, the map shows what happened in about three seconds:
  - the camera starts wide
  - coins rise over each station by what it earned while away
  - homes that grew rise in place
  - a full car park fills
  - then the camera settles on the station the player left

  The toast stays, shorter.
- **Optional.** Raise `MAX_AWAY` from half a game day to a whole one, so a phone player who comes back after lunch finds a day's takings.
- **Why.** Idle games make coming back the best moment of the session. Here it's a line of text. Serves: impacts on the map, phones first, incremental.
- **What it changes.** `src/main.ts`'s away handling and the view's existing rise animations. The sim is untouched unless the cap moves.
- **Risk.** None for perf. A longer cap gives more money while away, so the bot's pacing doesn't change, but a returning player's does.
- **Owner's call.** Whether to raise the cap.

### 5. Gridlock that keeps moving: the jam pushes people onto the trains, and local streets clear (improves the car, M)

- **What.** Two parts:
  - **G5 from the gridlock design.** Slow roads make driving feel longer, so more people take the train, and clear roads tempt some back.
  - **A local layer.** The streets round each owned station clear as its car park, bus stop and services come in, so every buy clears something near it, even while the M60 stays coral.
- **Why.** The bot shows the score barely moving after the opening (cars off 0.6 to 4%, the M60 fixed at 1.2). If the roads clearing is the score, it must keep moving. Serves: the car as the rival, impacts on the map.
- **What it changes.** `src/sim/traffic.ts` (G5 makes it feed back into demand) and the traffic view.
- **Risk.** G5 moves pacing, so it needs the bot. The local layer is view only.
- **Owner's call.** G5 was left for the owner to ask for; this review asks for it. The local layer needs no call.

### 6. Join the Bee Network (new, M)

- **What.** A network-wide stage chain, as Greater Manchester is really doing: the first rail lines join from December 2026, and most of Stockport's in 2027.
  1. Contactless tap-in, tap-out: barriers' share of fares at every station, and a quicker gateline.
  2. Bee Network yellow on the stations and trains: more trips from people who'd only take a bus.
  3. One fare across bus and train, with a daily cap: changing from a bus feels shorter, so the bus links bring more.
- **Why.** It's real, it's the region's story right now, and it ties trains and buses into one network, as the owner's "all public transport" asks. The yellow spreading over the map is a visible impact.
- **What it changes.** Fares and the bus links' change time, both in the sim. The third stage waits on buses as a network (phase 5 step 12).
- **Risk.** Pacing: the bot needs to measure it.
- **Owner's call.** None beyond the usual.

### 7. Feeder buses, a first slice before timetabling (reorders phase 5 step 12, M)

- **What.** Each bus stop the player owns gets one real feeder route to it, with a frequency to buy and bigger buses. This ships before the full bus network (step 12) and before timetabling (step 6).
- **Why.** Buses are the owner's direction, and stops are bought today but run no routes the player controls. A feeder per stop gives depth on today's map without new areas.
- **What it changes.** The order of phase 5 steps 6 and 12.
- **Risk.** Feeder buses on the map must be instanced; phones must hold the perf budgets.
- **Owner's call.** The reorder.

### 8. Match days at Edgeley Park and other surges you can see (improves Life, S)

- **What.** Stockport County at Edgeley Park, about 400 m from Stockport station, brings a surge on match days: fans in blue on the platforms, and extra trips to and from Stockport after the final whistle. Stepping Hill Hospital's shift changes at Woodsmoor, and Christmas shopping at Merseyway, do the same.
- **The crowd today.** Life already draws a seeded match-day crowd in club colours, but it changes nothing.
- **Buys that help.** Longer trains, extra units and the platform extension.
- **Why.** It gives a predictable peak to prepare for: a reason to buy before something happens rather than after. Serves: alive, impacts on the map.
- **What it changes.** Demand in the sim (seeded), and Life's crowd.
- **Risk.** Small for pacing if surges stay local.
- **Owner's call.** None.

### 9. Contract terms: targets for each contract (improves renewal, S to M)

- **What.** Each contract after the first comes with two or three targets from the franchise: a punctuality level, cars off the A6, Marple reached. Each met target banks extra reputation.
- **Today.** The milestones are recorded and nothing reads them.
- **Why.** It gives each run a shape without choosing a scenario, and uses what's already built.
- **Risk.** Little.
- **Owner's call.** Do targets count as an advisor? This review's view: no, because they say what the franchise wants, not what to buy.

### 10. Billboards on the jammed roads (new, S)

- **What.** A cheap, repeatable buy: a poster campaign for a station, shown as billboards along the A6 or M60 near it. While it runs, the station draws a few more people who'd drive.
- **Why.** It gives a small thing to buy in quiet stretches, aimed at the car. Serves: incremental, the car as the rival.
- **Risk.** It could become spam; keep one campaign a station at a time.
- **Owner's call.** None.

### Also noted, smaller

- **The bot never buys lifts, retail, a third unit or the second longer trains.** Pacing doesn't measure them. Whoever next touches the bot should add them to its policy, so the baselines cover every buy.
- **The Network card could grow with the network.** Punctuality, cars off, homes grown, and a small sparkline of takings by day, as figures.
- **The opening view at 06:30** is dark olive under a low sun on a phone. The gridlock opening changes this scene anyway; worth a look then.
- **The tram to Stockport.** Real: the extension from East Didsbury is at strategic outline case, with construction aimed at 2030. It stays in phase 7 under depth before breadth. If the owner wants a far goal on show, a fogged tram stop by Stockport station with its price would be the one exception.
