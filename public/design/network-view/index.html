A network view you can read

Whole line on a phone should answer “how’s my network?” at a glance. Above a height, the 3D map gets a Mini Metro layer: the services bold in their colours, trains as short bars by how full they are, stations as rings by who’s waiting, red where people give up. Drawn from the real line and the bot’s game on seed 1 at day 6’s morning peak. View only: nothing in the game changes.

What’s drawn

  • When: fades in as the camera goes from 2,500 m to 5,000 m from where it looks, so Whole line always has it and a station view never does. The clouds fade out as the camera rises from 900 m to 1,400 m.
  • Lines: one ribbon a service, 6 px wide on screen at any zoom, 7 px apart where services share track, from the service’s own route. Colours are tokens (--map-svc-*).
  • Trains: a short bar each, 15 by 9 px along its line, white below 60% of seats, yellow to 90%, coral beyond (--map-full-*).
  • Stations: a ring from 12 px to 36 px across by who’s waiting; its edge red with a slow pulse while 5 or more have given up this clock hour (Strain’s own figure); still with reduced motion. A station not yet bought: a small pale dot under its fogged name and price.
  • Taps: a ring or a train opens the card as a tap on the name or the train does now.
  • Cost: three instanced meshes (lines, rings, trains), loaded after the first frame, a few hundred triangles; hidden and not updated at any nearer zoom.

Whole line today

Day 6, the morning peak, from the bot’s game on seed 1. Whole line on a phone: the clouds at 520 m sit between the camera and the ground, the tracks are hairlines, and nothing says that Levenshulme has lost 92 people in ten minutes or that the stoppers are full.

The network view

The same moment with the view. Above about 1,400 m the clouds have faded out, and each service is drawn bold in its own colour, side by side where they share track. Trains are short bars coloured by how full they are: white with seats free, yellow busy, coral full. Each station is a ring as big as the crowd waiting on it; red, with a slow pulse, while people are giving up there. One glance: Stockport is crowded, the stoppers north of it are full, and Levenshulme and Ardwick are losing people.

With Strain on

Strain’s glows sit under the rings and keep their least size on screen, so they read at this height as they do close in; the figure on a strong one stays. The rings stay on top, so a tap still lands on the station.

Tap a ring

A tap on a ring opens the station’s card at half height, as a tap on its name does now; a tap on a train opens the train’s service. The camera then goes to the station as it does now.

Zooming in

Between about 2,500 m and 5,000 m from the ground the view fades: the lines thin and go translucent, the clouds come back, and the 3D map takes over as the kits come into range at 9,000 m. Shown here half way.

What it costs

Everything repeated is one instanced mesh: the ribbons are one mesh of quads a service; the rings and the trains one instanced mesh each, sized in the vertex shader so a frame writes only their places and colours, on the UI’s quarter-second timer for the rings and every frame for the trains. Fetched after the first frame, so the opening script doesn’t grow.
